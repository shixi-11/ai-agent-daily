The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

01Gemini Decision Multimodal Decision ModelUS and UK2026-11-11 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Gemini Decision Separates Classification From Generation

What happened: Google released Gemini Decision and the Decisions API on November 11. The specialized model returns probabilities over text, images, audio, video, and PDFs without generating output text. One request can evaluate up to 1,000 independent yes/no, single-choice, or rubric-rating questions. The shared input and all questions must fit within a 262,144-token context window.

Why it matters: Routing and scoring need not run through a text-generation loop. A dedicated endpoint makes latency, calibration, and business thresholds distinct engineering decisions.

Who should care: Teams building multimodal classifiers, support routing, policy checks, and agent tool selection.

Try it

Start with the official Decisions Playground or v1beta examples and compare predictions on a small labeled dataset.

Keep in mind

Zero output tokens does not mean free inference. Text generation, function calling, batching, and caching are unsupported. Validate calibration on your own data; we have not benchmarked it.

02Qwen4-Max-Preview Subscription PreviewChina2026-11-11 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Qwen4 Preview Is Limited to Token Plan Subscribers

What happened: QwenCloud introduced Qwen4-Max-Preview on November 11, combining text, image, and video understanding with tool calling and structured outputs. It lists a 1M-token context and a 393,216-token output budget shared by reasoning and the final answer. Access is limited to Token Plan; pay-as-you-go is unavailable.

Why it matters: This is a window for multimodal evaluation and migration testing, not a stable production commitment: the provider explicitly schedules retirement after the preview.

Who should care: Token Plan developers and teams testing long-context coding or visual agents.

Try it

Use the dedicated Token Plan endpoint and qwen4-max-preview model ID. Budget output tokens for both reasoning and the answer.

Keep in mind

The preview runs from 10:00 a.m. on November 11 through the end of November 25, Beijing time (UTC+8), after which the model will be retired. It is neither GA nor an open-weight release. max_tokens limits combined reasoning and answer tokens. We have not tested it.

04AWS AgentCoreUS2026-11-11T15:13:16-08:00 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

AgentCore Exposes Web Search as a Standalone API

What happened: AWS introduced a direct Web Search API for AgentCore, without first creating a Gateway or MCP target. SDK calls query Amazon’s own index and return ranked results with text extracts. Domain and publication-date filters are supported. Authentication uses IAM and SigV4, with optional PrivateLink connectivity.

Why it matters: Search no longer requires adopting an entire agent gateway. Existing orchestration and data pipelines can integrate it directly, while Gateway remains an option for shared policies and multiple tools.

Who should care: Teams with AWS applications, custom agent loops, or fact-checking pipelines that need web search.

Try it

Start with a narrow domain-filtered query from the official example and compare extracts with the original pages. Consider Gateway when several tools need shared administration.

Keep in mind

Titles, URLs, and dates are returned only when the index has them. CloudTrail data events require explicit configuration and omit query text and filters. We have not tested the API.

09Google Labs TabletopGlobal2026-11-11T16:00:00+00:00 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Tabletop Turns Group Plans into Shared Interactive Tools

What happened: Google Labs introduced Tabletop, a shared space that turns links, files and prompts into interactive itineraries, polls, expense splitters and tournament brackets. Participants can use the same space together through an invitation link.

Why it matters: It turns a conversation into an interface the group can operate together, rather than another summary to copy into a spreadsheet.

Who should care: People organizing family trips, recreational leagues or group meals.

Try it

Eligible US adults can join the waitlist and start with a small real collaboration task.

Keep in mind

Access is via a US 18+ waitlist, not global general availability. We have not tested it.

10ElevenAIGlobal2026-11-11 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: ElevenAI Connects Dictation with Meeting Follow-through

What happened: At its New York Summit, ElevenLabs previewed ElevenAI, using Scribe v3 for dictation and meeting notes. With participant consent, it produces summaries and attendee action items. The company says free early access opens that day.

Why it matters: The useful step beyond transcription is preserving who needs to do what after a conversation.

Who should care: Enterprise teams handling interviews and everyday meetings.

Try it

Check early-access eligibility on the official site and evaluate notes from a meeting with participants’ consent.

Keep in mind

Early access does not mean every capability shown at the summit is available. We have not tested it.

12ZumungoGlobal2026-11-11T21:58:28+00:00 demonstrated2026-11-12 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: Zumungo Turns Home Automation Requests into Reviewable Rules

What happened: Zumungo runs on a home computer and lets users draw rooms, discover devices and describe automation events or control panels in natural language. Its website says generated events are shown before confirmation, with optional cloud AI or local AI on an NVIDIA system with at least 16 GB VRAM.

Why it matters: It aims to simplify cross-brand configuration while separating generated rules from approval to save them.

Who should care: Households already using smart-home devices across several apps.

Try it

Check hardware and device support, then inspect a simple lighting rule first.

Keep in mind

Free software does not include hardware or third-party cloud-model charges. Compatibility and local operation were not tested. November 11 is the author’s showcase date.

Brief: Run Time-Series Forecasts Inside Postgres

What happened: tsfm-pg runs Chronos-2 and Timestral inside a Postgres extension, returning forecast quantiles through SQL. Models stay on the database host rather than sending series to a cloud API. The author showcased it today.

Why it matters: Forecasting stays close to stored data, but shares database compute resources.

Who should care: Teams storing metrics or business time series in Postgres.

Try it

Check build and model requirements in an isolated database first.

Keep in mind

We have not tested it. A single forward pass is not interruptible; do not assume production readiness.

11Open Hash LMGGlobal2026-11-11T21:30:07+00:00 demonstrated2026-11-12 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 2License GPL-3.0

Hash LMG Lets Small Models Storyboard While the App Builds the Film

What happened: Open Hash LMG splits production between a model and an application. A small local model writes a storyboard and simple drawings; the app handles layout, timing, motion and sound, with checks for text size, overflow and reading time. Its README links Apple Silicon downloads and GPL source, and lists export formats including MP4.

Why it matters: Rule-based layout and rendering reduce what a small model must solve, while leaving a timeline for manual editing.

Who should care: Independent creators making product shorts and kinetic typography locally.

Try it

Read the repository and try a short brief, then inspect text, pacing and exports.

Keep in mind

November 11 is the author’s Show HN announcement, not the first release. Development dates to October 13. Binaries, quality and full offline operation were not tested.

03AlphaGenome 2 Cell-Conditioned Genomic ModelUS and UK2026-11-11 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

AlphaGenome 2 Conditions Variant Predictions on Cell State

What happened: DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2 on November 11. It combines a million-letter DNA sequence with a specified cell type or state to predict variant effects on gene regulation. The model was trained on more than 100 million cell profiles. It is available through the AlphaGenome API for noncommercial research, with commercial research in private preview.

Why it matters: Moving beyond tissue averages can expose regulatory effects that appear only in particular immune states or disease-relevant cells.

Who should care: Researchers studying genetic variation, single-cell biology, and disease mechanisms.

Try it

Read the paper and API guidance, check research terms and supported inputs, and compare predictions with established experimental findings.

Keep in mind

TenK10K results are dataset-specific evaluations, not clinical diagnostic accuracy. Predictions need experimental validation. API access is not an open-weight release; commercial access has separate eligibility.

05Meta Intelligent DeliveryUS2026-11-11T17:00:38Z published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Meta Agents Refine Compute Allocation

What happened: Meta described how Intelligent Delivery allocates compute across regions, hardware, and ranking stages based on expected value per request. Agents read source code, design documents, and years of experiment logs to propose changes and design experiments. Engineers approve deployment. Meta says agents now contribute about 80% of engineering effort on its most automated mechanism.

Why it matters: These agents refine the serving system rather than directly controlling every ad request. Separating online allocation from proposals, experiments, and human approval clarifies where automation operates.

Who should care: Infrastructure teams optimizing inference capacity, recommendation costs, heterogeneous hardware, or engineering experiments.

Try it

Map the article’s regional, hardware, and host-level controls to your own parameters and constraints. Have an agent propose reproducible experiments before assessing system-wide latency effects.

Keep in mind

The 80% figure applies to engineering effort on one mechanism, not all development. The roughly 10% ranking gain accumulated from 2017 through the first half of 2026. A unified real-time model remains exploratory.

06Human-Agent X-rayUS2026-11-06T08:33:55Z published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Experiment Transcripts Reveal Decision Provenance

What happened: A synchrotron X-ray study records human–agent and agent–agent exchanges to trace decisions across three nanoparticle experiments. The authors report that 59–68% of corrections involved multiple participants as human-defined goals evolved.

Why it matters: Collaboration and correction paths become inspectable research evidence.

Who should care: Researchers studying autonomous labs and multi-agent teamwork.

Try it

Read the audit method and distinguish instrument logs from decision provenance.

Keep in mind

This preprint reports three experiments, not general reliability for autonomous laboratories.

08Sentence TransformersGlobal2026-11-11 published2026-11-12 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Train Retrieval Models for Vector Pooling

What happened: A Sentence Transformers tutorial incorporates pooling into the training objective. In the author’s experiment, a fourfold reduction in document vectors retained 97.8% of the pooling-aware model’s unpooled NanoBEIR nDCG@10, versus 94.6% of the conventionally trained model’s own unpooled score.

Why it matters: Compression can shape training rather than remain a post-training storage step.

Who should care: Engineers optimizing multi-vector retrieval indexes.

Try it

Compare retrieval at an equal vector budget, then test on your own corpus.

Keep in mind

The ratios describe specified models and NanoBEIR experiments, not total storage or speed gains.

In perspective: A larger model need not handle every step. Decisions, generation, experiments, and approval each have a place. Explicit handoffs reveal both the work saved and where the costs move.
