RISC Machine Primer

RISC = the four systems that make an agent production-ready

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Robust — BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, one failure can erase progress.
I | Intelligent — BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, uses tools, and completes complex work.
S | Secure — Immune SystemObject-capability security, policy-based approvals, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius.
C | Connected — SocialCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to one product.

AI Agent Radar

Approval

Muse and commerce demos expose approval points

Muse pauses for sensitive actions; Claude’s commerce examples stage merchant writes. Both make human control part of the workflow rather than a separate promise.

Execution

Inference tools target different bottlenecks

DeepSelect specializes TopK, Cohere reorganizes decoding, and DeepJIT shares compilation infrastructure. Their gains cannot be collapsed into one universal speedup.

Evidence

Evaluation tools expose different kinds of error

Pinocchio checks supported calculations, ClientCoded computes reference answers, and WearableQA tests reasoning on noisy records. Each makes a different failure mode visible.

New Features Worth Watching

01Intern-S2ChinaPublished 2026-09-13 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

Intern-S2 releases its official BF16 weights

What changed: Shanghai AI Laboratory released the official BF16 weights for Intern-S2-397B in 188 shards. FP8 files were uploaded on September 11. The model card describes scientific multimodal reasoning, tool use, and long-horizon agents, with deployment guidance for three inference frameworks.

Why it matters: The verifiable change is the release of the official weights, not a rebranding of the earlier preview. Researchers can now inspect the artifacts, licensing, and deployment requirements before running their own evaluations.

Who should care: Scientific-computing researchers and teams deploying large multimodal models.

Try it

Read the model card and deployment guide. Compare BF16 and FP8 hardware requirements before testing a small set of your own scientific questions.

Keep in mind

The model card specifies Apache-2.0. Reported capability scores are vendor evaluations, not validated scientific findings. This is not a lightweight desktop model.

02Meta MuseUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

Meta Muse gives personal agents a dedicated virtual machine

What changed: Meta introduced Muse with a gradual US rollout. It runs inside a dedicated Muse Secure VM with its own browser and can keep working toward longer-term goals. Sensitive actions, including sending email and making purchases, require approval. Users can interact through its app or WhatsApp.

Why it matters: As assistants move beyond individual conversations, the execution environment and approval points become central product decisions.

Who should care: Teams building personal assistants and consumer agents that work across applications.

Try it

Check regional availability in the official announcement. Examine the scope of access, approval requirements, and how control returns to the user.

Keep in mind

A gradual US rollout does not mean global availability. Shop Pay, 1Password, and some privacy enhancements remain planned features.

07PinocchioGlobalPublished 2026-09-10 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

Pinocchio gives spreadsheet numbers a traceable calculation history

What changed: The Pinocchio 0.1 research preview preserves sources, computation steps, and verification results in spreadsheet outputs. For supported numerical calculations, the system retains the source operands and records the steps so a deterministic verifier can replay the calculation. It currently handles one task at a time and exports xlsx files.

Why it matters: For numerical work, this turns a generated answer into an inspectable chain of inputs and operations rather than a prose explanation alone.

Who should care: People working with quotations, budgets, operational spreadsheets, and reconciliations.

Try it

Try a small, nonsensitive spreadsheet and inspect the source cells or sentences and the operations behind individual numbers.

Keep in mind

A Google account is required. Verification covers supported numbers, not all prose or judgment, and does not establish that every hallucination has been eliminated.

09AgentSporkGlobalPublished 2026-09-13 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

AgentSpork experiments with an agent help board

What changed: The creator demonstrated a public board for agents to ask for help, respond, and discuss tools. Visible conversations form an exploratory collaboration experiment.

Why it matters: It makes cross-agent interaction publicly observable.

Who should care: Developers studying how agents interact.

Try it

Read the discussions first and separate actual responses from the creator’s ambitions.

Keep in mind

Model identities can be self-reported. Activity is not evidence of effective collaboration.

10ClientCodedGlobalPublished 2026-09-13 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

ClientCoded simulates business environments for agent testing

What changed: The founding team demonstrated an agent QA platform using synthetic business data to test queries, ambiguity, and scope boundaries. Reference answers are computed with SQL.

Why it matters: It offers repeatable comparisons beyond manual spot checks.

Who should care: Teams preparing business agents for deployment.

Try it

Inspect the public environment examples and how reference answers are generated.

Keep in mind

These are not official environments from the SaaS vendors and do not replace real-world acceptance testing.

03DeepSeek DeepSelectChinaPublished 2026-09-10 / Observed 2026-09-14Official code releaseGitHub Stars 327License MIT

DeepSelect targets specific TopK bottlenecks

What changed: DeepSeek released DeepSelect v1.0.0 and an algorithm walkthrough for TopK operations in sparse-attention indexing and sampling. Vendor tests report a 2–20× speedup over torch.topk, with explicit constraints on data types, vocabulary size, stride alignment, and topk values no greater than 4096.

Why it matters: The optimization targets concrete workloads rather than every use of TopK. Its practical value is in reproducing gains at a measured inference bottleneck.

Who should care: Inference-engine developers and engineers working on sparse attention or GPU kernels.

Try it

Read the algorithm guide, then test representative input shapes. Check the rules for sorting, alignment, and NaN handling.

Keep in mind

The reported 2–20× gain applies to specific kernel tests, not end-to-end model inference. It cannot be assumed for larger topk values or different workloads.

04DeepSeek DeepJITChinaPublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-14Official code releaseGitHub Stars 300License not declared

DeepJIT unifies the runtime workflow for CUDA and Ascend

What changed: DeepSeek publicly released DeepJIT, a lightweight, header-only C++20 JIT runtime for CUDA GPUs and Huawei Ascend NPUs. Both backends share a compile, cache, load, and launch workflow, with memory and disk caches, lazy initialization, and PyTorch integration.

Why it matters: Kernel libraries can share runtime infrastructure rather than maintain it twice. The common interface does not make device code portable; each backend still needs its own compiler and launch options.

Who should care: Engineers maintaining cross-hardware kernel extensions, compilation caches, and inference infrastructure.

Try it

Start with the official examples and cache-key design. Shared storage must support atomic renames and file synchronization as documented.

Keep in mind

History-based cache warmup and direct compilation from Python remain under development. CUDA and Ascend kernel sources are not automatically interchangeable.

05Cohere MegakernelCanadaPublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-14Official code releaseGitHub Stars 85License Apache-2.0

Cohere keeps model decoding inside a persistent kernel

What changed: Cohere published a research inference engine for North Mini Code. A persistent CUDA kernel organizes the full decoding forward pass, reducing launch and synchronization overhead. Single-H100 tests include continuous batching and an OpenAI-compatible API, with reported end-to-end gains of 1.25–1.41× over vLLM v0.24.

Why it matters: The approach improves execution scheduling and memory utilization without changing the model, showing another route to lower-latency inference at small batch sizes.

Who should care: Engineers optimizing inference latency, CUDA scheduling, and model serving.

Try it

Match the documented model, GPU, software versions, and batch settings before comparing complete requests rather than decoding alone.

Keep in mind

Tuned for North Mini Code on one H100, not general use. Prefill still runs separate kernels and pauses decoding.

06Claude Commerce AgentsUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-01 / Observed 2026-09-14Official code releaseGitHub Stars 2,812License Apache-2.0

Claude commerce examples separate shopper and merchant authority

What changed: Anthropic published reference implementations for shopping and merchant agents, with retail, travel, telecom, and entertainment demos. Shared prompts, skills, and tool contracts run on the Messages API, Agent SDK, and Managed Agents. Merchant writes are staged for human approval.

Why it matters: Shopping advice and back-office changes carry different responsibilities. Separate roles, tool contracts, and approval surfaces make those boundaries easier to inspect.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams developing commerce assistants, merchant portals, and approval steps.

Try it

Run the fictional-data demos first. Compare shopper and merchant tool boundaries before reading the backend-integration and deployment guidance.

Keep in mind

The demos do not place real orders, charge cards, or change live listings. The examples lack authentication; deployers must implement their own business authorization and compliance rules.

08LearnlanceGlobalPublished 2026-09-13 / Observed 2026-09-14Official code releaseGitHub Stars 4License MIT

Learnlance turns AI code changes into a learning map

What changed: The author demonstrated Learnlance, which extracts concepts from agent-written code changes and generates a browsable HTML knowledge graph.

Why it matters: It offers a way to revisit the ideas behind generated code.

Who should care: Learners who want to understand code written with AI.

Try it

Read the README and try mapping concepts in a small project.

Keep in mind

Local graph storage does not mean all model calls stay offline.

11SwobuGlobalPublished 2026-09-13 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

Swobu puts model routing behind a local switchboard

What changed: The author demonstrated Swobu, a terminal interface for routing across model providers. Routes can be shared over HTTPS with another device and later revoked.

Why it matters: It separates client choice from backend-provider choice.

Who should care: Developers who switch among model providers.

Try it

Read the routing, sharing, and revocation guidance, then test with nonsensitive requests.

Keep in mind

Fallback after a runtime failure is not a compatibility check performed in advance.

12RunBothGlobalPublished 2026-09-13 / Observed 2026-09-14Official announcement

RunBoth compares behavior before and after AI edits

What changed: The author demonstrated RunBoth, which compares Python code behavior on matched inputs to reveal differences that may be hard to spot in a textual diff.

Why it matters: Output changes on the same inputs offer evidence beyond visual similarity.

Who should care: Developers using AI to modify Python code.

Try it

Read the README and test a small function with a known behavioral difference.

Keep in mind

FSL is not OSI-approved. Sampling is no guarantee of correctness.

13WearableQAUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-04 / Observed 2026-09-14Official research

WearableQA tests reasoning over wearable records

What changed: WearableQA has 4,084 questions with ten answer options each. Based on wearable records and related data from 200 real users, it tests reasoning about numbers, health, and multiple signals.

Why it matters: Real noise and missing records bring evaluation closer to everyday data.

Who should care: Teams studying health-data interpretation and model evaluation.

Try it

Read the paper and dataset documentation, including the serialization comparisons.

Keep in mind

Research and benchmarking only. CC BY-NC and additional terms prohibit use in clinical decisions.
