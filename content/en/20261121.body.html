RISC Machine Primer

RISC = the four systems that make an agent production-ready

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience — BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, one failure can erase progress.
I | Intelligence — BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, uses tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security — Immune SystemObject-capability security, policy-based approvals, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius.
C | Connectivity — Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to one product.

New Features Worth Watching

01OpenAIUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-17 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

GPT-6.2 Targets Professional Deliverables and Lower Task Costs

What changed: OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra for difficult projects and Sol for everyday work in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API. Standard Astra pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Sol retains rates of $2 and $10, respectively. Both target better documents, code, and multistep execution.

Why it matters: Output quality alone is no longer enough to compare models. Token use, revision rounds, and total cost per accepted task belong in the same evaluation.

Who should care: Teams producing professional documents, maintaining code, or running multistep business workflows.

Try it

Compare both models on the same sanitized files and established templates. Track total tokens, editing time, and acceptance rates in a reversible workflow.

Keep in mind

Capability and cost comparisons come from OpenAI and the evaluations it cites, not tests by this publication. Ultrafast has separate access and billing conditions; enterprise access depends on administrator settings.

02Google DeepMindUnited States / United KingdomPublished 2026-11-18 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Gemini 4 Neon Brings Long Workflows to a Million-Token Context

What changed: Google released Gemini 4 Neon with a one-million-token context and four thinking levels for coding, knowledge work, and science. Standard API rates are $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Google highlights context compaction, mid-task corrections, and orchestration of Hydrogen subagents.

Why it matters: Context size matters less if an agent cannot recover after instructions change. Neon and GPT-6.2 both shift attention toward the cost of completing an entire workflow.

Who should care: Long-session coding, cross-document analysis, and multi-agent teams.

Try it

Use Neon in AI Studio or the Gemini API with fixed tasks, tools, and budgets. Compare recovery, elapsed time, and total cost across thinking levels.

Keep in mind

Public rankings and internal safety tests use different configurations and do not establish production returns. The cyber-specific offering is rolling out separately through the Fairwind Program.

03Google DeepMindUnited States / United KingdomPublished 2026-11-19 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Genie 4 Opens Interactive Worlds Through a Paid API

What changed: Project Genie opened Genie 4 to eligible Ultra subscribers on November 18, with live world editing and native audio. On November 19, Genie 4 became generally available in the paid Interactions API, streaming 24 fps video with synchronized stereo audio. The app also adds a creation agent and community gallery.

Why it matters: The news is access and interaction, not a repeat of the earlier model announcement. Creation is moving from a generated vista toward environments that can change during exploration.

Who should care: Story, game, and experience designers; world-model researchers.

Try it

Check eligibility on the Project Genie site, remix a gallery world, and test whether repeated edits and camera changes preserve the scene.

Keep in mind

The app rolls out to eligible Ultra subscribers, with a Pro waitlist. The API is a separate paid entry point. Latency, complex physics, and long-term consistency remain limitations; this is not a full game engine.

04MicrosoftUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-18 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Microsoft Opens Aion Ahead of Its Windows Integration

What changed: Microsoft released the roughly 2.3-billion-parameter Aion 1.0 Instruct model under MIT. It supports eight languages, a 32K context, and on-device summarization and rewriting. This release is unquantized. A further optimized version paired with a moderation model is planned for Windows by the end of January 2027.

Why it matters: Downloadable weights and default operating-system availability are different milestones. Developers can evaluate offline workflows before integration while measuring the limits of a small model.

Who should care: Windows-app, offline-assistant, local-text, and deployment teams.

Try it

Review the official custom code in isolation. Then test summaries and rewrites across languages on multi-page documents.

Keep in mind

This is not yet the built-in Windows release, and quantized versions are still planned. Microsoft identifies limitations in knowledge and complex reasoning; local execution does not make decisions reliable.

05MetaUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-12 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

MetaEncoder Combines Retrieval With Closed-Set Decisions

What changed: Meta published MetaEncoder v1 weights and wrapper code for encoding, retrieval, and decisions across text, images, video frames, and document images. Its decision interface retrieves over candidate answers and returns probabilities and confidence. The model page recommends one 80GB GPU for BF16 weights.

Why it matters: Not every decision needs a generated explanation. A shared representation can shorten classification and routing paths, but decision probabilities still need calibration against business data.

Who should care: Teams building multimodal retrieval, document understanding, routing, and decision APIs.

Try it

Test retrieval and closed-set decisions separately with the official wrapper. Calibrate on labeled examples and retain a generative fallback for open questions or uncertain results.

Keep in mind

The weights and code use Apache-2.0. This is not a general chat assistant. Its recommended 80GB GPU setup is substantial, and probabilities need calibration for each task.

06Tencent WeMMChinaPublished 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

WeMM Supports Local Audiovisual Analysis

What changed: Tencent released 9B and 4B GGUF bundles for WeMM-Video, with a native runtime for text, images, audio, and video. The 9B bundle separates language, vision, and audio components. Examples cover frames alone or video with its soundtrack, CPU execution, and CUDA offloading.

Why it matters: Local video understanding depends on media encoders and a matching runtime, not just language-model size. Publishing the components together makes audio-aware deployment easier to reproduce.

Who should care: Developers exploring local video search, meeting-media understanding, and multimodal devices.

Try it

Download all three GGUF components at the same revision. Run the official short-video example, then compare answers with and without the original soundtrack.

Keep in mind

The Apache-2.0 license does not guarantee smooth execution on every device. The 9B files total more than 13 GiB and retain some BF16 tensors. Performance needs independent testing.

08Gemini APIUnited States / United KingdomPublished 2026-11-19 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Gemini Defers Tool Schemas Until They Are Needed

What changed: Tool Retrieval is in preview in the Gemini Interactions API. Names and descriptions stay visible while parameter schemas load on demand, with retrieval and tool calling in one request.

Why it matters: Large tool libraries need lower input overhead without worse tool selection.

Who should care: Agent platforms connecting large tool collections.

Try it

Enable deferred loading, then compare total input usage and tool-selection accuracy.

Keep in mind

This is a preview. Names and descriptions still consume context; the library is not free of overhead.

12Shanghai AI LaboratoryChinaPublished 2026-11-13 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Sage Connects Scientific Inputs to General Agent Workflows

What changed: Shanghai AI Laboratory released Sage S2.1 Flash and an FP8 version for coding, office work, tools, and research. Sage SciPerception encoders and projectors map molecular graphs, crystal structures, protein sequences, and other scientific inputs into a shared representation alongside natural language.

Why it matters: Scientific agents need research objects, not just paper summaries. Check the input pipeline before trusting a general benchmark score.

Who should care: Researchers in materials, life science, scientific computing, and multimodal agents.

Try it

Load one task projector using the official inference guide. Verify preprocessing and model versions before testing small samples with known answers.

Keep in mind

The model and code use MIT, but encoders, preprocessing, and task-specific readouts need separate setup. Author-reported benchmarks do not make scientific conclusions reliable.

13Alibaba QwenChinaPublished 2026-11-16 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Qwen4-Max Expands Multimodal API Access

What changed: QwenCloud listed Qwen4-Max on November 16 for text, image and video understanding, reasoning, coding, and agent tasks. It supports tools, structured output, explicit caching, and reasoning summaries. The November 11 Preview was available only through Token Plan; access conditions must be checked separately.

Why it matters: A preview and a production model listing are different milestones. Teams can now evaluate the listed interface but still need to confirm regional and account availability.

Who should care: Existing Qwen integrations, cross-model gateways, and multimodal application teams.

Try it

Follow the official release entry to the Qwen4-Max documentation. Check region, pricing, and fields before comparing tools and structured output on fixed examples.

Keep in mind

This confirms an official API listing, not an open-weight release. Do not transfer Preview access conditions or infer unpublished performance from the name.

14OpenAI GPT-LiveUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-19 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

GPT-Live-1.1 Adds Voice Standby

What changed: GPT-Live-1.1 is available through the Live sessions API with improved multilingual pronunciation, accent consistency, and transcription. WebRTC sessions support standby and natural-language guidance on when to delegate to a backend.

Why it matters: Voice-agent cost and responsiveness depend on what happens during silence as well as speech.

Who should care: Real-time assistant, customer-service, and multilingual application teams.

Try it

Compare pronunciation and transcripts on fixed multilingual scripts, then test speaking again after standby.

Keep in mind

Standby does not end the session. Check the official documentation for scope and charges.

A Browser Model Paints Four Tiny Emoji

What changed: The author created the Slack Moji Space on November 16. A text description produces four 16×16 emoji with Tessera-small running locally through WebGPU.

Why it matters: A tiny canvas turns local generation into an approachable creative toy.

Who should care: Pixel-art enthusiasts and browser-AI developers.

Try it

Open the Space in a WebGPU-capable browser and compare four interpretations of the same description.

Keep in mind

The model is CC BY-NC 4.0 and restricted to noncommercial use. It was not tested by this publication.

VoxNotes Connects Wrist Recording to On-Device Transcription

What changed: The author presented VoxNotes on November 20, linking Apple Watch recording with an iPhone timeline, transcripts, and speaker separation. Its site says speech recognition and voiceprint processing stay on device.

Why it matters: Voice capture is shifting attention from long summaries back to easy recording and retrieval.

Who should care: Interviewers, voice-note users, and local-first note takers.

17Cooked Yet?GlobalPresented 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Guess the Photo, Then Inspect the Generation Recipe

What changed: The author presented Cooked Yet?, a daily ten-image game asking whether each picture is real or an AI recreation. Answers reveal the original, generated twin, prompt, model, and other players’ choices.

Why it matters: Seeing where your own judgment fails is more useful than memorizing visual detection rules.

Who should care: Visual creators, media educators, and casual players.

Try it

Play the ten daily rounds, then revisit mistakes using the disclosed prompts and models.

Keep in mind

This is a game, not a general authentication tool. The presentation date is not the original product launch date.

19MiniMax H3 Image PEChinaPublished 2026-11-10 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

MiniMax Publishes Separate Image Prompt-Expansion Weights

What changed: MiniMax released FP4 H3 Image PE weights, task prompts, and a client on November 10. Fine-tuned from Qwen3.8-27B, it expands short text-to-image requests and recommends an aspect ratio. Image-editing PE remains in development; that workflow currently provides a prompt only.

Why it matters: Intent expansion can be deployed and inspected separately from image generation. Explicit descriptions and ratios make unintended changes easier to trace.

Who should care: Developers building local image pipelines, prompt management, and generation quality controls.

Try it

Run short requests through SGLang or Modular Diffusers and inspect whether expansion invents unwanted subjects, styles, or text.

Keep in mind

This catches up on the separate PE component, not the previously reported image model. It uses a custom community license, not OSI open source. Editing PE is prompt-only; roughly 19 GiB of GPU memory is an official estimate.

07THUDM CompactionRLGlobalPublished 2026-11-17 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party sourceGitHub Stars 0License Apache-2.0

CompactionRL Trains Agents’ History Summaries

What changed: CompactionRL released code on November 17. Agent actions and history summaries share the final task reward, with an asynchronous training stack, agent framework, and rollout buffer.

Why it matters: A summary becomes a trainable action that affects later success, rather than an external patch.

Who should care: Long-horizon agent and reinforcement-learning researchers.

Try it

Read the training semantics and fixed task manifests before examining credit assignment for summary tokens.

Keep in mind

The paper predates this code release. Results are reported by the authors and were not reproduced here.

A Community Port Connects Depth Pro 2 to ROS 2

Why it matters: Moving depth estimation into robots makes calibration and runtime interfaces central concerns.

Who should care: Robotic-perception and edge-vision developers.

Try it

Check the repository license and camera assumptions before using the demo on representative scenes.

Keep in mind

This is not an official Apple deployment. Code is Apache-2.0; the original weights allow noncommercial research only.

09OpenAI MedChemBenchUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

MedChemBench Separates Molecular Reading From Prediction

What changed: MedChemBench uses 99 problems to assess molecular structures, experimental explanations, candidate ranking, and property prediction, drawing on 117 papers and patents and 761 molecular structures.

Why it matters: Reading a structure correctly does not establish which compound should be tested next. Component scores matter.

Who should care: Drug-discovery and scientific-agent evaluation teams.

Try it

Inspect component scoring and examples before choosing metrics for a specific research stage.

Keep in mind

This tests reasoning over historical evidence, not the discovery of new drugs or better laboratory decisions.

10OpenAI Performance FactoryUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-19 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

Performance Factory Verifies Fixes After Deployment

What changed: OpenAI described its internal Performance Factory: alerts create tasks, agents investigate and propose fixes, engineers review, and an agent resumes after deployment to verify that performance has recovered.

Why it matters: A completed reply is not a resolved regression. Durable task state connects investigation, review, and verification.

Who should care: Operations, performance-engineering, and agent-runtime teams.

Try it

Map the case onto an existing alert workflow and identify the state needed while waiting for review or deployment.

Keep in mind

This is an internal case study, not a complete open-source release.

11NVIDIA RTX SparkUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-18 / Observed 2026-11-21First-party source

RTX Spark Compact Desktops Go on Sale

What changed: ASUS, Dell, and MSI RTX Spark desktops are now available with up to 128GB of unified memory for local models, always-on agents, and image or video workflows.

Why it matters: Persistent inference gains a dedicated desktop option without tying up a portable computer.

Who should care: Local-deployment developers and creative studios.

Try it

Check the specific configuration, software compatibility, and complete memory needs of the target model.

Keep in mind

Configurations and availability differ by vendor. Local capabilities and vendor performance claims need separate checks.

This issue’s takeaway: Measure total task cost, revision rounds, and verifiable outcomes together to distinguish model gains from workflow improvements.
