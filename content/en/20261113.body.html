The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

01Gemini 4 HydrogenUS and UK2026-11-12 published2026-11-13 observedOfficial primary source

Gemini Hydrogen’s Shared Million-Token Window

What happened: Google made Gemini 4 Hydrogen generally available through its API. It accepts text, images, video, audio, and PDFs and returns text. Its unified input/output window is 1,048,576 tokens. Default synchronous output is capped at 65,536 tokens; long decode extends that limit within the shared window. It also becomes the default model for the managed Antigravity agent.

Why it matters: Input and output share a budget; the headline limits cannot be added together. For coding and tool workflows, migration depends on output modes, tool support, and costs as well as model capability.

Who should care: Application developers and agent platform teams handling long documents, multimodal input, or multistep coding.

Try it

Try your own long documents in AI Studio using synchronous mode first. Before enabling long decode, check the protocol, remaining context budget, and costs.

Keep in mind

This is a closed-weight GA API, not an open-weight release. Image generation, speech generation, and the Live API are unsupported. A longer output limit does not guarantee quality; we have not benchmarked it.

02Claude Managed AgentsUS2026-11-12 published2026-11-13 observedOfficial primary source

Claude Managed Agents Adds Service Self-Hosting

What happened: Anthropic launched self-hosted Claude Managed Agents. Beyond hosting sandboxes, customers can run the entire service on their own infrastructure, retaining agents, sessions, and session history there. Servers still send requests to the customer’s chosen model provider, Anthropic by default. Access requires contacting an account team.

Why it matters: The service, execution environment, and model inference are separate deployment layers. Enterprises can relocate session data and control services without moving model weights locally; procurement needs to examine each layer.

Who should care: Platform and governance teams with requirements for session-history storage and infrastructure control.

Try it

Use the self-hosting documentation to map service, sandbox, storage, and model-request paths. Confirm eligibility with the account team rather than assuming sandbox hosting covers the entire service.

Keep in mind

This is request-based enterprise access, not general availability. Self-hosting does not open Claude’s weights or imply offline inference. We have not obtained deployment access or tested it.

Brief: AxiomStory Links Manuscripts to Character Art

What happened: AxiomStory reads manuscript chapters, flags continuity conflicts, and builds character and location wikis. Approved looks and voices carry into scene images and video. Its author introduced it on Show HN.

Why it matters: The same manuscript provides character references across tools, reducing repeated descriptions.

Who should care: Novelists and creators developing character-driven stories.

Keep in mind

The free allowance is one-time and excludes images and video. We have not tested consistency.

06Survive DinnerGlobal2026-11-12 demonstrated2026-11-13 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: Talk Your Way Through a Dinner with Nosy Relatives

What happened: Survive Dinner lets players choose a situation, such as being single, between jobs, or starting a company, then select three relatives. Spoken answers affect table tension and post-dinner judgments. The author introduced it publicly.

Why it matters: Voice agents can organize reactive role-play rather than simply answer questions.

Who should care: Players interested in short role-playing games and spoken interaction.

Try it

Choose a situation and guests on the website, then review microphone prompts.

Keep in mind

The first game needs no signup. Further play requires login and payment for extra dinners. We have not played it.

07Animate4MeGlobal2026-11-12 demonstrated2026-11-13 observedCreator’s primary source

Animate4Me Exports Editable Spine Rigs

What happened: Animate4Me takes character art or prompts and generates layers, hidden-area fills, bones, weighted meshes, and physics constraints. It exports Spine 4.2 JSON, atlas, and PNG texture files rather than only a video. The website offers a live browser rig and a downloadable sample. Its author introduced the product publicly.

Why it matters: Editable output can enter a game-asset workflow. File compatibility, runtime versions, and licensing determine whether developers can actually ship the animation.

Who should care: Independent developers and artists making 2D character idles, prop effects, and interactive assets.

Try it

Inspect the live rig and download the sample to check Spine 4.2 compatibility. Then try an idle template with your own character and inspect hidden areas and mesh deformation.

Keep in mind

Generation uses paid tokens, with plans starting at $19 per month. The character win template is still marked coming soon; start with idle. Using an official Spine Runtime in your product requires your own Spine license. We have not paid for a generation test.

03SGLang-NeuronUS2026-11-12 published2026-11-13 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 8License Apache-2.0

SGLang Brings Its Serving Interface to Trainium

What happened: AWS announced a public preview of SGLang-Neuron, released by RadixArk with Annapurna Labs. The out-of-tree plugin retains SGLang’s server interface and generation APIs on Trainium. It uses native PyTorch, torch.compile, and NKI kernels, and supports disaggregated prefill and decoding with KV-cache transfer over NIXL and EFA.

Why it matters: A hardware move need not require a new application interface. Separating chip execution from framework scheduling helps, but model support, validated configurations, and workload results still determine portability.

Who should care: Teams evaluating Trainium serving, heterogeneous inference, or reinforcement-learning rollouts.

Try it

Start with the official container and a supported instance. Check the example model against your existing API, then measure your own concurrency, context lengths, and costs.

Keep in mind

The walkthrough uses Trainium3 and a specific container. A shared API does not guarantee identical model or feature coverage. We have not deployed it or established a GPU performance advantage.

08Cerulion 2.0Global2026-11-12 published2026-11-13 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 108License AGPL-3.0

Cerulion Adds Verified ROS 2 Process Replay

What happened: Cerulion 2.0 adds exact replay logs for a single ROS 2 process, retaining consumed-input order, wait results, and output hashes. Reruns compare outputs and identify the first divergence. The release also changes default LAN pairing and the node ABI; existing node libraries require rebuilding.

Why it matters: Replaying messages is different from rerunning code. Recording what a process actually consumed and comparing outputs helps distinguish behavior changes from merely viewing sensor data again.

Who should care: Robotics teams investigating failures, validating control-code changes, or maintaining ROS 2 tests.

Try it

Record the official obstacle-avoidance example, rerun it in isolation, and inspect the verdict. Change a control parameter to test whether it identifies the first output divergence with matching inputs.

Keep in mind

The core uses AGPL with commercial licensing; hosted MCP and other components have separate closed-source terms. Replay publishes outputs, so keep it away from live robots. Unsupported cases, including multithreaded executors, may be refused.

09RealtimeVADGlobal2026-11-12 demonstrated2026-11-13 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 51License MIT

Brief: RealtimeVAD Triggers Earlier Speech Processing

What happened: RealtimeVAD adds audio-level checks every 10 milliseconds alongside Silero. An early pause event can trigger speculative generation. The author introduced the alpha release and benchmarks from personal conversation recordings.

Why it matters: Speculation trades more false triggers for earlier response preparation.

Who should care: Engineers building local real-time voice assistants.

Try it

Compare pause and end events using your own 16 kHz mono recordings.

Keep in mind

A pause event can fire between words and should trigger only reversible work. Reported gains are the author’s results.

04AgentCore RuntimeUS2026-11-12 published2026-11-13 observedOfficial primary source

AgentCore Raises Session Limits and Reclaims Memory

What happened: AWS updated AgentCore Runtime while retaining a separate Firecracker microVM for each session. Sessions now support up to 8 vCPUs, 32 GB of memory, and 10 GB container images. AWS says sessions start in under two seconds. The update also adds memory reclamation, idle scale-to-zero, and filesystem mounts across multiple regions.

Why it matters: Long-running agents do not compute continuously. Separating waiting costs from peak execution resources makes task economics clearer, while extending controls to working files.

Who should care: Enterprise platform teams deploying code execution, browser automation, or long-lived conversational sessions.

Try it

Compare cold starts, memory billing while waiting, and storage permissions on an existing workload. Verify that sessions cannot access each other’s files before widening a migration.

Keep in mind

Startup and efficiency figures are AWS claims, not our measurements. Per-session storage scoping through session-labeled credentials is planned by re:Invent; it is not available yet.

In perspective: Generation is not the end of the workflow. Runtime costs, service boundaries, and editable formats determine what can happen next. At each handoff, look for a result you can inspect yourself.
