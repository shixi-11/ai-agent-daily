The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Voice specialization

Voice Models Take Different Jobs

Qwen audio manages live turns, Nemotron handles long recordings, and Homi returns probabilities for multiple-choice questions about audio. The input may be speech in each case, but latency, context, and output formats make them different tools.

Operating conditions

Inference Gains Depend on Hardware

DeepInfer specifies supported accelerators and missing service components; Andromeda separates production throughput from isolated measurements. Performance figures inform deployment only when read alongside those system boundaries.

Trying things out

Interactive Projects Expose Their Limits

The garden puts agent activity in a public space, while Harness downloads a model into the browser. Sharing is not privacy, and local execution does not mean every device can play.

01Google DeepMindUS and UK2026-10-28 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary source

Cybersecurity Partners Get Carbon First

What happened: Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, targeting cybersecurity and long-horizon engineering. Access starts this week with selected cyber defenders, then expands through the Fairwind Program to trusted partners. Broad access for developers, businesses, and consumers is still to come.

Why it matters: The focus extends beyond isolated test scores to sustained, multistep problem solving. Capability announcements and access are proceeding separately, so benchmark results and usable entry points need separate scrutiny.

Who should care: Technical teams evaluating long-running coding, cyber defense, and enterprise model access.

Try it

Review the capabilities and limitations; eligible Fairwind participants can watch for access details. Evaluate error recovery and evidence quality on long tasks.

Keep in mind

The reported cyber benchmarks were evaluated without guardrails, not under equivalent production conditions. Announced introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, not a permanent rate.

02QwenCloudChina2026-10-27 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary source

Qwen Adds a Flash Model for Live Voice

What happened: QwenCloud released qwen-audio-3.1-realtime-flash on October 27, with duplex streaming speech, text input and output, function calling, and voice cloning. Its documentation covers semantic turn detection and target-speaker enhancement to distinguish pauses, interruptions, and background voices.

Why it matters: Voice assistants depend on when to speak and whom to listen to, not just transcription accuracy. Explicit turn and speaker controls give customer-service and companion products more concrete behavior to test.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams building live voice support, interactive devices, and multilingual companions.

Try it

Test pauses and competing voices with the official examples, then choose WebSocket, WebRTC, or AOQ. Measure latency and mistaken interruptions as well as answer quality.

Keep in mind

Web search and function calling cannot be enabled together. The release log lists a 262,144-token window, but audio history has separate limits of 50 turns and 300 cumulative seconds; it does not preserve an unlimited recording.

03NVIDIAUS2026-10-28 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary source

Nemotron Makes Long-Form Transcription Promptable

What happened: NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe weights on October 28, combining transcription, translation, speaker attribution, and audio Q&A. It transcribes 40 languages and handles recordings up to an hour. Instructions can retain fillers, change written formatting, or supply domain vocabulary; fine-tuning materials are also available.

Why it matters: Speech-to-text no longer has to produce one fixed kind of transcript. Choosing verbatim text, readable notes, or speaker-attributed records helps connect outputs to interviews, meetings, and media search.

Who should care: Teams building multilingual captions, meeting records, audio search, and specialized transcription tools.

Try it

Use two styles from the model card's prompt table on the same short recording. Compare omissions, names, and timestamps, then check memory and NeMo Speech requirements before deployment.

Keep in mind

This is an offline recording model, not a real-time streaming ASR service. Weights use OpenMDW-1.1. The fastest throughput comes from NIM-specific optimizations that NVIDIA says are not yet part of the open-source release.

07VercelUS2026-10-28 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary source

Virtual Models Centralize Model Changes

What happened: Vercel introduced AI Gateway Virtual Models on October 28. Teams call a fixed ID and change its underlying model, fallback order, and settings such as Zero Data Retention centrally, without code changes or redeployment. Virtual-model settings override matching request options, preventing callers from removing central constraints.

Why it matters: Model selection becomes a task configuration rather than a string scattered across applications. That simplifies migration and rollback, but one configuration change can also affect many entry points.

Who should care: Platform teams managing model access, cost attribution, and data-handling rules across applications.

Try it

Create a virtual ID for a low-risk task and a candidate ID for comparisons. Check provider options before switching, then monitor success rates and task costs.

Keep in mind

Changes take minutes to propagate. Vendor-specific options do not transfer automatically. Evaluate task quality even with centralized routing.

04egma.ai HomiGlobal2026-10-28 demonstrated2026-10-29 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 3License Apache-2.0

Homi Answers Audio Questions With Probabilities

What happened: Homi's creator demonstrated an audio decision model on October 28. It adds a decision head and LoRA to a frozen Qwen3-Omni backbone, returning probabilities for answer options instead of generating text. The repository includes inference, evaluation, training, and data-reconstruction code for questions about pace, pitch changes, emotion, and more.

Why it matters: Some acoustic information disappears in transcripts. The resulting probabilities can feed classification and monitoring systems directly, while making it easier to assess whether confidence matches accuracy.

Who should care: Developers studying audio quality control, voice-interaction evaluation, and batch audio analysis.

Try it

Start with the multiple-choice examples and recordings with known answers. Inspect probabilities and errors alongside calibration results, rather than treating accuracy as the whole story.

Keep in mind

The creator flags long multiparty conversations, speaker counts, and reasoning from pauses as weaknesses. BF16 needs about 70 GB of free GPU memory. Reported costs use H200 batching, not an ordinary desktop.

05TellersGlobal2026-10-28 demonstrated2026-10-29 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 2License MIT

Preview Video Edits While an Agent Works

What happened: Tellers demonstrated video-editing Skills on October 28. Coding agents can arrange cuts, layers, titles, effects, and music in an OTIO timeline. A local browser player previews the result and exports an MP4 into the project folder; a separate MCP plugin edits cloud projects and calls generation services.

Why it matters: An editable timeline lets people inspect an agent's decisions instead of waiting for a final video. Local preview and export also make the workflow easier to connect to existing media files.

Who should care: Creators and small teams using coding agents for screencasts, short videos, and product demonstrations.

Try it

Start with a few nonsensitive local clips. Ask the agent to create a timeline, adjust one cut, and preview it; check captions, music, and media paths before export.

Keep in mind

The local player needs no Tellers account, but cloud MCP requires sign-in. The player loads a Tellers-hosted script, so local preview does not mean fully offline. We did not export a video.

06DeepSeekChina2026-10-20 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 23,916License Apache-2.0

DeepInfer Opens Its Offline Inference Core

What happened: On October 20, DeepSeek released DeepInfer code for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, including optimized kernels, asynchronous scheduling, structured outputs, and multiple forms of parallelism. The Apache-2.0 repository provides source-build instructions and offline inference examples for NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs.

Why it matters: The release exposes execution details behind the model. From batch scheduling to deterministic sampling, teams can inspect how output stability and throughput are optimized together rather than comparing weights alone.

Who should care: Infrastructure teams studying large-scale serving, accelerator support, and reproducible inference.

Try it

Check the Linux, Python 3.12, and CUDA or CANN requirements, then review the offline examples. For batch-invariant inference, verify platform support and feature flags.

Keep in mind

The release excludes a complete KV cache system, service disaggregation, and data-parallel load balancing. Batch-invariant inference and deterministic sampling currently support NVIDIA only.

08MetaUS2026-10-28 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary source

Meta Moves Retrieval Into One GPU Pipeline

What happened: Meta detailed its next-generation Andromeda architecture on October 28. Index matching, mixture-of-experts scoring, and candidate selection run on one GPU server to reduce intermediate data movement. Meta reports production throughput above one billion ad candidates per second; the higher figure for the isolated scoring kernel measures a different workload.

Why it matters: As computation accelerates, transfers across networks and GPU memory can become the bottleneck. The lesson is joint model, kernel, and service design—not applying a component's speedup to an entire system.

Who should care: Architects and engineers working on large-scale recommendation, retrieval, and GPU systems.

Try it

Compare the scoring-kernel, index-matching, and end-to-end measurements. Map data movement in your own service and measure waiting time before choosing a model or deployment change.

Keep in mind

This is a production-architecture disclosure, not a downloadable general-purpose retrieval product. The figure above ten billion candidates per second refers to isolated scoring, not the billion-scale production pipeline.

13ByteDance SeedChina2026-10-25 published2026-10-29 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Recycler Reuses the Previous Hidden State

What happened: ByteDance Seed published Recycler research on October 25. A lightweight adapter passes the previous token’s hidden state into the next step, retaining information beyond discrete tokens. The official abstract reports reasoning and mathematics gains.

Why it matters: Passing continuous information across steps offers an alternative to simply enlarging a model.

Who should care: Teams studying architectures, training efficiency, and latent reasoning.

Try it

Use the official paper link to inspect training stages, baselines, and added overhead.

Keep in mind

We verified the research abstract; it does not establish a publicly available product API.
