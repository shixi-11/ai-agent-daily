RISC Machine Primer

RISC = the four systems that make an agent production-ready

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Robust — BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, one failure can erase progress.
I | Intelligent — BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, uses tools, and completes complex work.
S | Secure — Immune SystemObject-capability security, policy-based approvals, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius.
C | Connected — SocialCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to one product.

01OpenAI ChatGPT Images 2.5United States / GlobalPublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-09Official release

ChatGPT Images 2.5 Reads Sketches and References More Closely

What changed: OpenAI released ChatGPT Images 2.5, saying it turns written ideas, rough sketches, and reference photos into more personalized, polished images that better reflect user intent. The product moves incomplete visual direction into a more direct generation workflow.

Why it matters: Image agents are moving beyond one-shot generation. The differentiator is whether they can interpret partial intent while preserving relationships across several references, reducing the need to translate every visual choice into prose.

Who should care: Teams in branding, advertising, ecommerce, content, and multimodal products.

Try it

Use one rough sketch with three different reference sets. Keep the subject and layout fixed, vary only material and atmosphere, then compare identity, typography, and spatial drift.

Keep in mind

The capability description comes from OpenAI’s official RSS and product-page summary. This issue did not independently test prompt adherence, Chinese typography, pricing, latency, or multi-turn consistency.

02OpenAI Navier–Stokes ResearchUnited States / Global researchPublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-09Official research

OpenAI Publishes a Navier–Stokes Solution With a Lean Proof

What changed: OpenAI published what it describes as an AI-generated solution to the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem, alongside a written argument and a formal proof in Lean. The release pairs prose reasoning with a machine-checkable artifact for separate inspection.

Why it matters: High-value research agents are moving from proofs that merely read well toward objects a theorem prover can check step by step. If the approach holds up, peer review gains a replayable path for locating errors.

Who should care: Mathematics, formal-verification, research-agent, theorem-proving, and high-assurance software teams.

Try it

First compile the full Lean project. Then sample three pivotal lemmas, compare the prose claim with the formal definition and dependencies, and inspect the project for undeclared axioms.

Keep in mind

OpenAI calls the work an AI-generated solution and provides a Lean proof. This issue did not independently validate the complete mathematics, and publication does not mean the research community or Millennium Prize authorities have accepted the result.

03OpenAI Codex Quantum ExperimentsUnited States / Global researchPublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-09Official research

Codex Enters a Quantum Calibration Loop

What changed: OpenAI describes an MIT research workflow in which GPT-5.6 Sol and Codex autonomously run quantum-computing experiments, analyze results, and calibrate qubits. The agent does more than write code: it uses returned measurements to adjust the next operation.

Why it matters: Once a model can act on instrument feedback, the central problem is no longer script generation. It becomes constrained iteration: detecting anomalies, preserving state, recovering safely, and staying within human-defined authority.

Who should care: Quantum-computing, lab-automation, scientific-platform, instrument-software, and long-running-agent teams.

Try it

Map the case into planning, execution, measurement, diagnosis, and calibration. For each stage, mark automatic continuation conditions, human approval points, and thresholds that must stop the run.

Keep in mind

OpenAI’s official case study describes the workflow. This issue did not access the instruments or raw data and cannot independently assess success rate, experiment cost, reproducibility, or the degree of human intervention.

04Tencent EVIEChina / Global open sourcePublished 2026-09-04 / Observed 2026-09-09Official open-source release

Tencent Opens Two EVIE Visual Document Retrievers

What changed: Tencent released EVIE-8B and EVIE-4.5B for visual document retrieval. They retain layout, typography, chart, and table relationships through per-token multi-vector embeddings and MaxSim. The model card reports 66.75 nDCG@10 on ViDoRe V3 and provides weights, inference code, and evaluation tools.

Why it matters: A document agent can lose local structure when an entire page is compressed into one vector. Late interaction over multiple vectors gives queries a finer evidence path through contracts, reports, and knowledge bases.

Who should care: Enterprise-search, RAG, contract, financial-document, knowledge-base, and multimodal-retrieval teams.

Try it

Use pages with two columns, cross-page tables, and small footnotes. Compare retrieval with a single-vector baseline while recording memory use, index size, and query latency.

Keep in mind

Apache-2.0 weights and code are public. The 66.75 score comes from the official model card under its stated protocol; this issue did not reproduce it, and the result does not establish equal performance across languages or enterprise documents.

05OpenAI Agents SDK for PythonUnited States / Global open sourcePublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-09Official open-source release

OpenAI Agents SDK Moves Guardrails to the MCP Server Layer

What changed: OpenAI Agents SDK for Python v0.22.1 adds server-wide guardrails for MCP tools, configurable Unix-local environment isolation, Docker container labels, image results in web search, and streamed transcription options. It also fixes edge cases around empty tool arguments and approval resumption.

Why it matters: As tool counts grow, security checks added at every call site become easy to miss. Moving policy to the MCP server layer lets one capability entry point share controls and creates a clearer isolation and audit boundary.

Who should care: Teams working on agent platforms, MCP services, security, development automation, and voice workflows.

Try it

Apply one deny rule to a test MCP server, then call the same tool from two agents. Check blocked messages, traces, recovery flows, and local isolation settings.

Keep in mind

Features and fixes come from the official v0.22.1 release notes. This issue did not test sandbox escapes across platforms, and process-environment isolation should not be treated as a complete security sandbox.

06DeepSeek HarnessChina / Global open sourcePublished 2026-09-08 / Observed 2026-09-09Official open-source release

DeepSeek Harness Updates System Prompts Without Dropping the Cache

What changed: DeepSeek Harness v0.1.5-alpha.1 can update the system prompt without invalidating the KV cache when the model supports it. Its new session format records the system prompt in history, preserves old files during migration, and fixes the unintended resumption of paused goals and the ordering of inserted messages.

Why it matters: Long sessions need policy changes without recomputing the entire context every time. Keeping prompt changes, cache reuse, and migration records in one state chain can reduce cost while preserving traceability.

Who should care: Coding-agent, long-session orchestration, inference-infrastructure, and local-development teams.

Try it

Run a long session on a compatible model, change one system rule, and compare cache hits, time to first token, and recorded history. Then verify that a paused goal remains paused.

Keep in mind

This official alpha preserves cache across dynamic prompt changes only on supported models. Savings, migration rollback, and long-run stability were not independently tested.

11UnoUnited States / Global researchPublished 2026-09-03 / Observed 2026-09-09Official researchGitHub Stars 52License Apache-2.0

Uno Delivers Lossless Multi-token Generation via Discrete Diffusion

What changed: Uno combines autoregressive and discrete-diffusion weights, using Ψ-Spec to generate several tokens in parallel. The team released inference, training, and evaluation code with three checkpoints.

Why it matters: It aims to improve throughput without a separately trained draft model while preserving the autoregressive distribution.

Who should care: Inference-engine, model-training, and high-throughput-agent teams.

Try it

Reproduce throughput, time to first token, and output agreement on the same hardware and batch sizes.

Keep in mind

The reported speedup of up to three times comes from the authors. It depends on particular hardware and attention implementations and was not reproduced here.

12FlowBalanceUnited States / Global researchPublished 2026-09-03 / Observed 2026-09-09Official research

FlowBalance Grounds Self-distillation in Verifier Outcomes

What changed: FlowBalance uses verifier-derived advantage to set the update direction, adds token-level feedback from a sign-gated self-teacher, and learns a distribution over complete responses through trajectory balance.

Why it matters: Dense self-guidance cannot override the verified outcome, reducing the chance that teacher preference points training the wrong way.

Who should care: Reasoning-model, RLVR, self-improvement, and evaluation researchers.

Try it

Reproduce the five-benchmark average, then inspect response length, strategy diversity, and late-training degradation.

Keep in mind

Reported gains come from the authors’ Qwen3 4B and 8B experiments. They were not reproduced or extended to other models here.

13EmbodiedSkillsChina / Global researchPublished 2026-09-01 / Observed 2026-09-09Official research

EmbodiedSkills Adds Preflight Checks to Robot Actions

What changed: EmbodiedSkills sends a VLM through observation, planning, preflight, bounded execution, verification, and recovery before a skill request reaches the robot policy.

Why it matters: Embodied-agent reliability comes from pre-execution gates and outcome checks, not planning prompts alone.

Who should care: Robotics, VLA, lab-automation, and embodied-agent teams.

Try it

Reproduce the ablation without intermediate verification and compare failure modes and recovery counts.

Keep in mind

The authors report 86.20% on RoboTwin, while RMBench reaches only 12.5%. This issue did not reproduce either result.

14Split-LLM Privacy StudyEurope / Global researchPublished 2026-09-03 / Observed 2026-09-09Official research

Returned Gradients Reveal Real Rows in Split-LLM Training

What changed: The study finds that zero patterns in returned Split-LLM gradients distinguish real rows from decoys, succeeding on every row across nine random seeds in the authors’ setup.

Why it matters: Obscuring forward representations does not mean the backward update cannot reveal the same secret.

Who should care: Teams in privacy-preserving training, federated learning, and enterprise models.

Try it

Reproduce the attack on synthetic data, then test clipping, noise, and different batch sizes separately.

Keep in mind

The result covers only the authors’ threat model, and five other attack classes remain unmeasured. Generalizing it to every Split-LLM setup is not justified.

This issue’s takeaway: Model interfaces keep expanding, but production differentiation increasingly depends on whether state, evidence, and authority can be preserved and independently verified.
