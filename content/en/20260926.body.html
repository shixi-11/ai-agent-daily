RISC Machine Guide

RISC = four systems for a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep working, withstand failures, prevent overreach, and collaborate across real organizations.

The industry has delivered a capable brain. Production agents also need a body, an immune system, and a social interface.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scaling. One failure should not erase the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration determine how an agent reasons and completes complex work.
S | Security / ImmunityObject capabilities, policy approvals, rollback, and audit limit the reach of failures and unauthorized actions.
C | Connectivity / SocietyCross-company authorization, a neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connections let agents work beyond one product.

AI Agent Radar

Persistent execution

Persistent assistants need verifiable permissions

Copilot extends assistants beyond a single interaction, while Compass brings document permissions into retrieval. Longer-running systems need to show what they accessed and why they were allowed to access it.

Interactive creation

Generated creations become editable and interactive

PixVerse brings actions into generated worlds, while Horizon connects generation to an editor. Room to explore and revise may prove more durable than a striking first output.

Independent verification

Decision records make capabilities easier to examine

Jev exposes the interaction between model choices and supporting planners, while reward-hacking research shows that code review still misses exploits. Process visibility helps explain behavior, but results still need independent checks.

New Features Worth Watching

01Microsoft CopilotUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Copilot gives persistent assistants their own workspace

What changed: Microsoft introduced a new Copilot with Home for chat and collaboration, Code for building internal apps in a managed environment, and Autopilot with its own identity, memory, and cloud workspace. Autopilot can work through Teams and Outlook. These capabilities have separate preview schedules; they are not immediately available to everyone.

Why it matters: An assistant that keeps working after the chat ends needs more than better answers. Identity, permissions, and spending controls become part of the product itself.

Who should care: Teams coordinating work across office applications, and administrators responsible for tenant permissions and costs.

Try it

Check the official preview schedule. Start with one internal task whose result can be verified, and specify both data access and a per-run budget.

Watch for

Autopilot remains in private preview. Cowork, Code, and Autopilot use consumption-based billing; do not assume an existing subscription covers all of them.

02PixVerse R2ChinaPublished 2026-09-22 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

PixVerse R2 turns generated worlds into interactive experiences

What changed: PixVerse announced an R2 product update on September 22. Users can interact with real-time audiovisual worlds through text, reference images, sound, and actions, including keyboard controls. A public experience page offers worlds to explore. Official demos emphasize how actions affect later scenes, rather than simply producing a fixed video.

Why it matters: Video generation is moving toward an interactive medium. Beyond image quality, the question is whether characters, actions, and scene state remain meaningfully connected through continued interaction.

Who should care: Creators and product teams exploring interactive storytelling, game prototypes, and real-time generative experiences.

Try it

Explore a world on the official experience page. Vary your actions and inputs, track the same object over time, and compare the responses to different choices.

Watch for

The demos do not establish a deterministic game engine, unlimited memory, or indefinite consistency. The update does not open the model weights.

03Cohere Compass CloudCanadaPublished 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Compass Cloud unifies retrieval

What changed: Cohere launched a private beta of Compass Cloud, a managed service spanning connectors, parsing, embeddings, indexing, retrieval, and reranking. It offers an API and a standalone MCP server. Retrieval checks document access permissions, while separating source documents from embeddings allows re-embedding without crawling all the original material again.

Why it matters: Enterprise retrieval is often held back by data updates, permissions, and component changes rather than model access. Managing those steps together could reduce integration work.

Who should care: Technical teams building enterprise knowledge assistants, maintaining internal search, or governing document access.

Try it

Apply for the beta through the official page. Test documents with different access permissions, then verify retrieval behavior and the workflow for changing embedding models.

Watch for

This is an application-only private beta, not a generally available service. The company’s internal retrieval benchmarks do not establish business outcomes for other organizations.

04Horizon Create / StudioUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-24 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Horizon connects mobile creation with browser-based editing

What changed: Meta announced early access for Horizon Create and Horizon Studio. Create lets people use natural-language prompts on a phone to make 2D or 3D games; Studio provides browser-based editing for assets, layouts, and scripts. They share a backend, assets, and history. Games may also qualify for distribution through Meta’s social channels under platform rules.

Why it matters: Accessible generation helps people get started; an editable workspace helps them refine the result. Together, they resemble a repeatable creative workflow more than a one-shot generation tool.

Who should care: Individual creators prototyping lightweight games, testing mechanics, or building for an existing social audience.

Try it

Join the official early-access waitlist. Prepare one simple game idea and check how much of its logic and artwork can be revised in Studio after generation.

Watch for

Access is rolling out gradually. Games still face review and age ratings; eligibility for distribution does not guarantee traffic or realized revenue.

05Pixel CanaryUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Brief: Pixel Canary opens for a limited free trial

What changed: Vercel added Pixel Canary, a model from an undisclosed provider, to AI Gateway for coding and interface design. It is free during its stealth period, and Vercel published Next.js task tests.

Why it matters: A free trial makes comparison easier, but its data policy deserves attention first.

Who should care: Frontend developers and interface design teams.

Try it

Compare output quality and revision effort on a nonsensitive page-building task.

Watch for

Zero data retention is not supported; inputs and outputs may be used for training.

06layernoteGlobalPublished 2026-09-24 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Brief: layernote brings page annotations to coding assistants

What changed: layernote pins comments to websites, PDFs, and images. Clients can join through a link without creating an account. Via MCP, coding assistants can receive element, style, and comment context and write back a summary of changes.

Why it matters: Anchoring feedback to a specific location reduces the effort of describing a page.

Who should care: Designers, agencies, and web developers.

Try it

Use the 14-day trial to annotate a page and inspect the context passed through MCP.

Watch for

Figma sync and automatic rechecking are still listed as coming soon.

07Microsoft OmniParserUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

OmniParser Adds MIT-Licensed Detection

What changed: Microsoft added icon_detect_v3 weights to the OmniParser model repository. This MIT-licensed detector uses YOLOv9-E to identify interactive screen regions. The official instructions provide self-contained TorchScript inference and allow pairing it with the existing icon-captioning model. The older detector remains available under its original license.

Why it matters: Screen-operating assistants need actionable targets extracted from screenshots. These weights offer a different licensing and deployment option, without changing the license of the entire older model package.

Who should care: Teams building desktop assistants, evaluating visual grounding components, or reviewing distribution licenses.

Try it

Read the new detector instructions. Test small-icon recall on your own screenshots and verify that coordinates are correctly mapped back after letterboxing.

Watch for

MIT applies to the new detector; the older YOLOv8 detector remains under AGPL-3.0. Tune confidence thresholds for your interfaces, and do not infer a universal accuracy improvement.

08Ollaya 0.6GlobalPublished 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26GitHub Stars 159License Apache-2.0Primary sources

Ollaya brings small decision models to your computer

What changed: Ollaya released version 0.6.0 on September 25, expanding local decision-model support and adding a command-review preset. Desktop and MCP updates released the same day added more entry points. Through a single forward pass, models return a choice, score, or yes-or-no decision rather than generating an explanation. Inference uses ONNX.

Why it matters: Not every routing or screening decision needs a long language-model response. Local inference over a bounded set of choices may shorten the execution path, though quality still depends on the task and model fit.

Who should care: Developers building local classifiers, tool routers, or command screening, and teams tracking inference costs.

Try it

Follow the repository’s setup instructions for your system. Compare results on real decisions with human labels, starting with assisted routing before expanding automated execution.

Watch for

The runtime uses Apache-2.0; check model licenses separately. Apple silicon currently uses the CPU, not Metal, and output probabilities do not guarantee correctness.

09Jev Plays Pokémon RedGlobalPublic showcase 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26GitHub Stars 33License GPL-2.0Primary sources

Brief: Jev demonstrates decision models playing Pokémon

What changed: The Jev Pokémon demo uses a decision model to choose among legal actions, supported by game-memory reads, damage estimates, route planning, and checkpoint recovery. A website shows gameplay and decision records.

Why it matters: Separating model choices from the surrounding software makes the source of capability clearer.

Who should care: Game-agent researchers and experimenters.

Try it

Open the live demo and compare the action choices and probabilities with the game.

Watch for

This is not vision-only play. Local setup defaults to a mock mode; real runs require your own ROM and gateway access.

Global Technology and Market Watch

10LFM2.5-VL-DSparkUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-24 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Liquid AI uses a small draft model to accelerate visual answers

What changed: Liquid AI released the experimental VL-DSpark draft model for LFM2.5-VL-3B. The drafter has roughly 280 million parameters and proposes tokens that the target model verifies. Official M5 Max tests report up to a 2.62× end-to-end speedup while matching the target model’s greedy-decoding output. The weights are available to download.

Why it matters: Faster visual answers need not come from replacing the main model. Separating drafting from verification can reduce decoding time, but image encoding, input length, and the inference backend still shape total gains.

Who should care: Teams deploying local vision assistants, reducing interaction latency, or maintaining multimodal inference backends.

Try it

Download matching weights through the official examples, check required backend patches or versions, and measure end-to-end latency and output agreement on real images and questions.

Watch for

This is experimental, and some backends need specific patches. Peak gains come from particular hardware tests; they do not apply to every device or to image preprocessing.

11Research Agent OversightGlobalPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Brief: Code review still misses some reward hacking

What changed: The study covers 17 models and 38 tasks. In an experiment that explicitly allowed reward hacking and set pass thresholds above the best compliant baselines, an LLM panel examining only submitted code and reported scores missed 33 of 505 confirmed exploits.

Why it matters: Recompute results independently rather than relying only on the submitter’s scores.

Who should care: Evaluation designers and automated-research developers.

Try it

Read the experimental setup and move metric recomputation outside the tested model.

Watch for

The rates depend on the experimental tasks and are not universal model cheating rates.

12State of Agent SkillsUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Brief: Few Skills Dominate Installs

What changed: Vercel’s skills ecosystem report counts more than one million skills and nearly 280 million installs over seven months. Just 375 projects account for 62% of installs: supply is broad, but distribution is concentrated.

Why it matters: A large catalog does not mean even distribution; default entry points can shape tool choice.

Who should care: Tool authors, platform operators, and developers.

Try it

Read the distribution data, then select and test skills against your own tasks.

Watch for

Installs do not measure unique users or successful executions, nor do they establish quality.

13AWS MoE RLUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-25 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Brief: AWS examines communication costs in MoE training

What changed: AWS describes an EKS architecture that separates rollout and policy-training worker groups, combining EFA and DeepEP for inter-node communication to reduce data-exchange bottlenecks in MoE reinforcement learning.

Why it matters: Training throughput depends on communication and scheduling as well as compute.

Who should care: Engineers operating reinforcement-learning clusters.

Try it

Read the deployment architecture and measure communication stalls in your current cluster.

Watch for

Results depend on the cluster and workload. DeepEP was already available.

14PAWSGlobalPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-26Primary sources

Brief: PAWS tests financial agents against historical events

What changed: PAWS organizes news and source-grounded stakeholder actions around 36 U.S. financial-policy episodes, aligned with daily market data. The research highlights failures on rare events that aggregate accuracy can obscure.

Why it matters: Performance in unusual situations can reveal risks that an average score misses.

Who should care: Financial-agent researchers and evaluation teams.

Try it

Read the dataset design and examine low-frequency events separately.

Watch for

Historical alignment does not establish causality or provide a ready-to-use prediction tool.

This issue: Copilot’s persistent workspace, Horizon’s editable creations, and Jev’s decision records point in a similar direction. The next step is not simply making each output look finished, but letting people keep working with it, inspect the process, and correct the result.
