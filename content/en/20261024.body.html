The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

01KimiChina2026-10-24 published2026-10-24 observedOfficial primary source

Kimi Expands 3D Creation and Long-Horizon Work

What happened: Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro and a Highspeed variant with a one-million-token context window across its website, apps, Work, Code, and API. The announcement demonstrates coding, parametric modeling, and financial research. 3D Make offers online previews and editing. Full model weights are scheduled for release by November 4.

Why it matters: Model improvements now arrive alongside concrete creation workflows, from sketches to editable 3D assets. API access and weight availability are separate milestones: teams can try the service now, but cannot yet plan deployment around downloadable weights.

Who should care: Developers and professionals working on long coding tasks, 3D prototypes, or financial analysis across documents.

Try it

Test edits and exports with a small parametric model in Kimi. Alternatively, compare Pro and Highspeed through the API for completion time, cost, and output quality.

Keep in mind

Speed and benchmark results are vendor measurements, not guarantees for every task. Enterprise access requires administrator approval. Weights remain pending, and financial figures need verification.

02MiniMaxChina2026-10-23 published2026-10-24 observedOfficial primary source

MiniMax Moves From API Access to Open Weights

What happened: The official MiniMax M3.1 Flash repository now contains 48 main weight shards, a DSpark draft head, and local deployment instructions. An October 23 commit establishes the new weight availability. This is distinct from the October 19 API launch; a model-page update alone would not establish a release date.

Why it matters: Downloadable weights let teams investigate inference deployment and infrastructure compatibility beyond hosted APIs. Access does not mean inexpensive operation: model size, numerical formats, and supported engines still determine practical costs.

Who should care: Teams evaluating open-weight models, self-hosted inference, and multimodal agent infrastructure.

Try it

Read the official model card and local deployment commands. Check hardware, runtime, and weight formats before a small validation workload; do not assume older configurations apply.

Keep in mind

Weights use the custom MiniMax Community license, not an OSI-approved open-source license. We verified public files and instructions, but did not measure throughput or deployment costs.

03Tencent Youtu-LLM-A7B-BaseChina2026-10-21 published2026-10-24 observedOfficial primary source

Tencent Opens a Vision-Language Base Model with 7B Active Parameters

What happened: Tencent released weights and custom inference code for Youtu-LLM-A7B-Base. The vision-language mixture-of-experts model has about 120 billion total parameters, activates roughly 7 billion per token, and supports a 262,144-token context window. Its repository lists 24 weight shards plus MTP weights.

Why it matters: The release gives researchers a deployable pretrained foundation for text and image understanding. A smaller active parameter count does not mean the model needs only the memory of a 7B model: deployment still has to accommodate the full weights.

Who should care: Teams studying sparse inference, multimodal pretraining, and long context.

Try it

Read the model card, then test short text continuations and image inputs using Transformers or the repository’s dedicated SGLang adapter.

Keep in mind

This is a base model, not a ready-to-use chat assistant, and it uses a custom Youtu license. Loading requires trusting remote code. The model card warns against enabling overlap scheduling for high-concurrency image traffic.

04Block DessaCanada2026-10-23 demonstrated2026-10-24 observedCreator’s primary source

Dessa Learns to Listen and Speak From Audio

What happened: A small research team at Block demonstrated Dessa, a full-duplex speech model it says was trained directly on human conversation, without text or pretrained models. Recordings show interruptions, laughter, volume changes, and a conversation with GPT-Live. The site offers samples and a waitlist, with early access for a small group of safety researchers.

Why it matters: Speech carries more than words. Timing, emphasis, and turn-taking can become direct learning targets. The approach is worth watching, but natural delivery does not establish factual accuracy or emotional understanding.

Who should care: Developers, designers, and audio creators exploring real-time conversation and vocal expression.

Try it

Listen to the official two-track recordings and compare interruption and response timing. Check the waitlist separately; recorded demonstrations are not features you can necessarily use today.

Keep in mind

Audio-only training and local Mac operation are team claims, without public weights for reproduction. Joining the waitlist does not grant immediate access or establish a generally available assistant.

05AnthropicUS2026-10-23 published2026-10-24 observedOfficial primary source

Enterprise Claude Unifies Chat and Cloud Work

What happened: Anthropic made its unified chat and Cowork experience generally available for Enterprise. Users can ask questions or delegate projects in one conversation. Code and file work continues in isolated cloud virtual machines after a laptop closes. A new administrator-controlled Efficient mode limits model use to Sonnet and Haiku.

Why it matters: A unified entry point removes a tool-selection step while moving cost and access decisions into organizational settings. Teams still need to assess resource use, cross-device continuity, and preservation of existing enterprise controls.

Who should care: Enterprise administrators expanding assistants into shared document, analysis, and delivery workflows.

Try it

Inspect organization settings, then test cloud continuity with an interruptible file task. Compare Efficient mode with other models for output quality and usage.

Keep in mind

Organizations using specified zero-retention, HIPAA-ready, or customer-managed-key configurations retain their existing setup. Pricing remains usage-based; longer tasks can consume more tokens.

What happened: AgentProbe demonstrated fault-injection tests that connect to an agent endpoint. A second model chooses when to introduce tool errors, stale information, or missing or false success confirmations. Reports identify failure steps and costs.

06CleanYourTVGlobal2026-10-17 demonstrated2026-10-24 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 27,904License not declared

Coding Agents Tidy Up the TV Home Screen

What happened: CleanYourTV packages the author’s October 17 TV makeover into prompts, tools, and device lists. A coding agent uses Android Debug Bridge (ADB) over the local network to back up settings, disable unwanted software, and install a minimalist launcher. Android TV and Google TV are the main targets, with a restoration path retained.

Why it matters: This brings coding assistants to hardware already in the home, without a new AI device. The useful pattern is device discovery, backups, and stepwise verification—not copying one disable list onto every television.

Who should care: Hands-on users comfortable with device debugging who want less advertising and preinstalled clutter.

Try it

Read the device-specific list and restoration instructions. Check casting, voice, and update features you want to keep. Disable debugging access when it is no longer needed.

Keep in mind

Support is not universal: Tizen and webOS differ, and Fire TV functionality is limited. Disabling system components can break features; a community list is not a guarantee for every set.

07Photoshop MCPGlobal2026-10-23 demonstrated2026-10-24 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 0License MIT

Agents Edit Photoshop Images Layer by Layer

What happened: Independent project Photoshop MCP demonstrated control of Photoshop on a Mac through AppleScript. It can inspect layers, adjust color, select subjects, create masks, and export. Edits form individual undo steps, a checkpoint precedes the first change, and before-and-after previews and histograms return to the agent.

Why it matters: Unlike delivering only a flattened image, this bridge retains familiar layers and history. Language-driven edits become inspectable, but a person still needs to judge the intended composition. Automatic rollback does not replace an independent backup.

Who should care: Mac users with a Photoshop license who want to explore language-driven editing and batch work.

Try it

Install from source and use a disposable photo. Adjust exposure, inspect the result, and undo it to verify layer preservation. Photoshop 27.11.0 is the explicitly tested version.

Keep in mind

Generative fill, brushes, and 3D are unsupported. Previews may reach the model provider, so local execution does not keep all images local. Existing DMGs are not notarized.

09SomaGlobal2026-10-23 demonstrated2026-10-24 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 0License MIT

Brief: Agent Messages Carry Budgets and Signatures

What happened: Soma demonstrated message envelopes carrying goals, budgets, deadlines, and signatures, alongside tiered memory, a minimal reference implementation, and conformance tests. Protocol code is separate from commercial engines.

Why it matters: Agent collaboration can define verifiable message handling rather than merely exchange chat.

11Sketch2SimGlobal2026-10-20 published2026-10-24 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Hand-Drawn Circuits Become Computable Netlists

What happened: The Sketch2Sim paper describes recognizing circuit components, values, and connections in stages, then generating SPICE-compatible netlists to compute voltages and currents. The study curated 1,000 hand-drawn RLC circuits.

Why it matters: Image recognition extends to calculations with inspectable connections.

Who should care: Circuit educators and engineering-diagram researchers.

Try it

Read the pipeline and check each node against a simple circuit drawing.

Keep in mind

Treat this as research; we have not confirmed a broadly available online service.

12Scaling in GamesGlobal2026-10-20 published2026-10-24 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: More Game Worlds Do Not Ensure Transfer

What happened: A study of embodied game agents compares 120 training configurations, from 0.8B to 27B parameters and 50 to 1,600 hours of data. Diversity helps transfer to related worlds, with limited gains in distant ones.

Why it matters: More training data does not guarantee control across unfamiliar worlds.

Who should care: Researchers training game-playing and embodied agents.

Try it

Recheck your evaluations using the paper’s distinction between related and distant worlds.

Keep in mind

These findings concern continued pretraining, not every training method.

In perspective: An editable 3D model, preserved image layers, or an inspectable permission list is easier to assess than a broad capability claim. The next question is whether these interfaces behave consistently across devices, users, and unexpected failures.
