The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Division of labor

Models and Retrieval Allocate Effort

Fable can delegate simpler work to smaller models, while Bedrock lets applications choose retrieval depth. Avoiding unnecessary reasoning can improve the experience alongside stronger capabilities.

Execution checks

Industrial and Robot Systems Add Test Layers

AURA checks the structure of computed tables, while robot simulation exposes manipulation failures. Language judgments need interfaces to execution evidence that can be tested separately.

Persistent actors

Agent Roles Separate From Runtime Controls

Quick distinguishes triggers, actors, and capabilities. AIM is integrating Starfort’s detection capabilities with OpenShell’s runtime controls. A persistent actor does not need unlimited permission to act.

Fable and Mythos 5.2 Target Long-Running Technical Work

What happened: Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2. They share an underlying model, but Fable retains stronger cyber and biology safeguards, while Mythos requires organizational verification. Anthropic reports up to 30% faster output than 5.1. Standard API pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, with a separately priced fast mode.

Why it matters: The release distinguishes difficult technical work from everyday tasks. Fable can advise other models or delegate simpler work. Teams should compare total task cost and reliability, not just token prices.

Who should care: Teams handling sustained coding, scientific computation, and complex research, plus developers designing model delegation.

Try it

Check platform availability in the release notes. Compare a task with clear acceptance criteria against Opus 5.5, including completion rate, total cost, and human review.

Keep in mind

Fast mode costs $16 for input and $80 for output per million tokens. Mythos requires verification. Performance and long-running examples are vendor tests, not guarantees of unattended success.

02Bedrock Managed Knowledge BaseUS2026-11-09T12:13:18-08:00 published2026-11-10 observedOfficial primary source

One Retrieval API Now Supports Different Reasoning Budgets

What happened: Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base expanded AgenticRetrieveStream. A maxAgentIteration value of 0 returns passages without a language-model call; 1 retrieves once and generates an answer; 2–5 retain iterative planning and retrieval. Applications choose the mode for each request, from direct lookup to cross-document comparison.

Why it matters: Not every question needs a full agent loop. Separating passage lookup, single-shot answers, and complex investigation makes latency and model usage easier to control and explain.

Who should care: Developers building enterprise knowledge assistants, policy search, and answers spanning knowledge bases.

Try it

Test the same representative questions with 0, 1, and iterative settings. Measure evidence coverage and latency. For passages only, disable generateResponse and set the value to 0.

Keep in mind

The application selects the mode; the service does not automatically route questions. Setting generateResponse=true at 0 returns an error. The banking scenario is illustrative, not measured customer results.

04Nuance EmmaGlobal2026-11-09 published2026-11-10 observedOfficial primary source

Emma Makes Live Conversation More Expressive

What happened: Nuance Labs released its Nuplex 1.0 full-duplex audiovisual model and Emma persona, alongside a beta API. Users can have browser-based audiovisual conversations without an account. The system listens and speaks simultaneously, using gaze, expressions, and pauses to manage turns. Emma’s face and voice are licensed from an actress; this is not arbitrary face cloning.

Why it matters: Real-time interaction is about more than reply latency. When to interrupt, when to keep listening, and how to react visually are becoming model and product design questions in their own right.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams studying live voice, virtual personas, audiovisual interaction, and background tool work.

Try it

In an eligible region, try Emma and observe pauses, interruptions, and background noise. Developers can inspect how the beta API hands longer tasks to tools while conversation continues.

Keep in mind

Access is limited to adults, English, and specified regions in the US, Europe, and Canada; China is not included. Comparisons are vendor tests. Visual understanding, lip sync, and turn-taking can fail, and Emma cannot sing.

05VorkGlobal2026-11-09T03:43:46Z published2026-11-10 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 1,287README: vork Preview License; GitHub SPDX unrecognized; not open source

Vork Lets Agents Write Entire 3D Scenes as Code

What happened: Vork’s creator showcased a 3D and VFX engine for AI agents and released version 0.3.0. Its core API fits in about 2,500 tokens. Agents write geometry, materials, shaders, cameras, and physics, then inspect GPU renders and revise the code. Examples cover bird flocks, approximate black holes, and compositing, without driving an editor through clicks.

Why it matters: Executable scene descriptions make cameras, lighting, and effects editable and reusable as code. This gives creative agents another execution path, but an engine’s capabilities are not a substitute for visual judgment.

Who should care: Teams building 3D agents, procedural visuals, and VFX tools, plus creators comfortable with code.

Try it

Start with the repository’s init example. Set the camera and lighting, render a preview, and check GPU support for Metal or Vulkan ray queries.

Keep in mind

This is a source-available research preview, not OSI open source. The license restricts modified redistribution and hosted rendering. Speed comparisons do not establish equal quality; the black-hole example is not an exact relativistic solver.

03CrossnoKaye AURAUS2026-11-09T10:55:43-08:00 published2026-11-10 observedOfficial primary source

An Industrial Agent Separates Computation From Plant Control

What happened: AWS detailed CrossnoKaye’s AURA architecture. Agents read a shared plant model, control interlocks, and historical sensor data, then run Python in isolated microVMs. A deterministic harness checks output table columns and types, triggering a retry on failure. Base equipment control stays on local edge clusters, including during cloud outages.

Why it matters: Industrial diagnosis needs both equipment relationships and verifiable numbers. The useful pattern is a separation of language reasoning, checked computation, and established control logic—not a model directly turning valves.

Who should care: Engineers building industrial diagnostics, maintenance, and time-series agents, along with platform architects.

Try it

Read the session design: interactive work keeps a short-lived warm sandbox; scheduled reports start clean. Test output schemas, retries, and evidence references against offline historical data.

Keep in mind

Production and energy figures are customer-reported, not our measurements, and cannot all be attributed to the agent. Existing interlocks and edge safeguards are not replaceable by a general chatbot.

06NVIDIA at CoRLUS2026-11-09T14:00:01Z published2026-11-10 observedOfficial primary source

Robots Rehearse Delicate Work in Simulation

What happened: At CoRL, NVIDIA announced the GR00T 2 world action model and showcased delicate manipulation, industrial evaluation, and agent-written robot programs. Existing Isaac Lab 3.0 and GaP technology featured in the demonstrations: the former combines physics simulation and parallel training; the latter composes skills into readable programs that can be refined after failures.

Why it matters: Sequencing skills also requires identifying failures and deciding when to retry. Simulation supplies practice for delicate tasks, while readable programs let engineers inspect the intended control flow before physical deployment.

Who should care: Research and engineering teams working on robot learning, industrial assembly, physics simulation, and embodied agents.

Try it

Watch the cable, strawberry-deformation, and tool-sorting demonstrations, then follow each model and benchmark release page. Design perturbations and failure cases for your task rather than comparing successful videos alone.

Keep in mind

GR00T 2 is announced, but we have not verified downloadable weights. Isaac Lab 3.0 and GaP were already public. NVIDIA says the Arena 0.4 developer preview is arriving at CoRL. Demonstrations and benchmark success rates do not establish field reliability.

What happened: AWS shared Quick’s taxonomy: Triggers specify when work starts and what it involves. Agents are persistent actors; tools, skills, and workflows supply capabilities, with learning fed back from sessions.

Why it matters: A scheduled instruction need not become a new agent.

Who should care: Teams designing agent products and orchestration interfaces.
