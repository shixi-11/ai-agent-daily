The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Team mechanisms

Agent Coordination Becomes Reproducible

Agensh enables task claiming and merging, Bellman defines session sharing, and Shotstash records job progress. The common question is not how many windows are open, but who is responsible for each step and how the results are verified.

Everyday creation

Creative Tools Preserve Editable Steps

Loböle retains parameters, BakeVideos retains scenes, and the shared painting retains discoveries. Continued control after generation lets a one-off output become work people can build on.

Verification methods

Local Tools Expose Practical Checks

Mantid compares simple baselines, Narro distinguishes local and cloud voices, and the speech framework breaks down delays. Evaluation should focus on data flows, output quality, and actual cost, not merely the presence of AI.

04MantidGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary source

Mantid Turns Local Training Into a Data Workflow

What happened: Mantid demonstrated a local training app for Apple Silicon. After importing tables or documents, it suggests answerable questions and trains predictive models or customized language models. It compares results with simple baselines, retains versions, and offers model export and local MCP access. A free download is available on its website.

Why it matters: Lowering the barrier means more than hiding training commands. The useful sequence is to ask whether the data supports a question, then check whether the model beats a simple guess. Small teams can identify models that are not worth deploying instead of mistaking complexity for value.

Who should care: Small teams with business tables that want to explore local prediction without mastering machine-learning tooling.

Try it

Test a clear target with de-identified sample data. Hold out examples from training, and inspect the simple baseline, error patterns, and version rollback.

Keep in mind

According to the website, the app requires an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or later) running macOS 14 or later, with 8 GB of memory for conventional models and 16 GB for language models. Local data processing is a vendor claim; anonymous usage analytics are enabled by default.

05The Neverending PaintingGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary source

A Shared Painting Turns Every Zoom Into Creation

What happened: The Neverending Painting was publicly demonstrated as a shared AI artwork that visitors can keep zooming into. When visitors explore an unseen area, an image model paints what lies deeper and preserves it in the collective work. The site says exploration is free without an account; signing in lets visitors receive credit for discoveries and name locations.

Why it matters: Generation becomes a shared act of exploration rather than a prompt followed by a single download. The interesting questions are how new imagery relates to existing scenes and whether persistence turns a passing encounter into a place people can revisit.

Who should care: Creators and designers interested in generative art, interactive storytelling, and collaborative interfaces.

Try it

Open the painting in a browser, zoom gradually into one detail, then return to compare the path. Explore how the shared work is organized before registering.

Keep in mind

Free exploration and permanent storage are statements from the author’s site; we have not tested long-term retention. Discovery credit does not confer exclusive rights over the entire shared work.

06TributaryGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary source

Follow Musical Influences to Find Your Next Song

What happened: Tributary demonstrated a listening site that traces influences behind favorite artists. Its creator used Claude and ChatGPT to help find and summarize interviews and criticism, then paired songs by artists and their predecessors. Source links, decade browsing, and a random entry point help listeners compare the works directly.

Why it matters: Recommendations can point to an artist’s own account, not just stylistic similarity or shared audiences. AI acts as a research assistant here. Paired songs and original citations leave room for listeners to form their own judgments.

Who should care: Listeners and musicians seeking fresh playlists, stylistic history, or creative references.

Try it

Pick an artist you know, open a song pair, and read the citation. Follow the influence chain while distinguishing explicit statements from a critic’s stylistic comparison.

Keep in mind

These are curated relationships, not a complete music history. AI-assisted research can miss context. Playback availability and external service restrictions depend on the actual page.

07Whistle TranslatorGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: Offline Dictation Translates Into the Text Field

What happened: Whistle Translator demonstrated local dictation and translation on Mac and Windows, with seven spoken input languages and eight translation targets. It also records meetings and processes WAV files. Downloaded models support offline use.

Why it matters: Speech, translation, and typing become one action without repeated app switching.

Who should care: Desktop users who dictate across languages.

Try it

Compare a short transcript with its translation, then check insertion in the target app.

Keep in mind

The app is unsigned. Chinese is not supported; check the language list first.

Brief: Automated Videos Retain Scene-Level Editing

What happened: BakeVideos demonstrated a pipeline from an idea to script, voice, visuals, and captions. Users can rerecord one scene, replace footage, or adjust pacing. The site remains labeled pre-launch and offers a trial entry point.

01Microsoft AgenshUS2026-10-24 published2026-10-25 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 42License MIT

Microsoft Opens a Framework for Self-Organizing Agent Teams

What happened: Microsoft released Agensh, its experiment launch scripts, and a reproduction guide on October 24. Agents claim tasks, verify results, and merge work asynchronously through shared repositories, messages, and context, without a central orchestrator. Demonstrations scale to 1,024 agents, while the main five-task averages compare teams of one to 128 agents.

Why it matters: Multi-agent research is releasing reproducible coordination methods, not just demonstrations of more workers running at once. Shared progress and integration matter. Whether extra parallelism pays off still depends on pass rates, elapsed time, and total inference costs.

Who should care: Developers studying multi-agent software engineering, team coordination, and reproducible experiments.

Try it

Read the replay and reproduction guide, then compare one agent with a small team on the same task. Track pass rates, tokens, and integration conflicts before scaling up.

Keep in mind

Results are limited to specified models, six-hour budgets, and experiments without Internet access. The thousand-agent example is not an average improvement across all tasks, nor does it imply unchanged cost.

02LoböleGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 2License AGPL-3.0

Parametric CAD Gives People and Agents the Same Controls

What happened: Loböle demonstrated version 0.1 of its parametric CAD application. Its interface, Python scripts, and agents use one command layer for sketch constraints, assemblies, drawings, and version branches. The repository provides JSON-RPC, a Python SDK, and MCP access, with examples including an engine, a watch, and a bicycle.

Why it matters: Language-driven modeling connects to editable solid geometry rather than a rendered picture alone. A shared command layer makes automated steps easier to compare with manual work. Engineering validity still requires dimensional, constraint, and simulation checks.

Who should care: Developers and makers exploring language-driven modeling, parametric design, and engineering interfaces.

Try it

Begin with a simple bracket or drilled part. Change an upstream dimension, inspect downstream features, and export STEP geometry for verification. Use the examples to learn the command layer.

Keep in mind

This is early software, with Linux first. Windows lacks finite element analysis, and macOS is untested. Machining has been validated on only one machine. The remote interface lacks authentication; do not expose it directly to the Internet.

03NarroGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 3License MIT

Local Browser Voices Read Kindle Pages Aloud

What happened: Narro demonstrated a Chrome read-aloud extension with sentence highlighting, on-device text recognition for Kindle web pages, and automatic page turns. Users can select browser-based Kokoro, Supertonic, Piper, or Kitten voices, or separately connect cloud speech services.

Why it matters: Reading assistance need not send an entire document to the cloud by default. Local voices, reading positions, and page controls form a practical tool. But the selected engine determines where data goes; offline support does not make every configuration local.

Who should care: Desktop readers who want to listen to long articles and books and are willing to check extension permissions.

Try it

Choose a local engine for a public article. After downloading its model, disconnect from the network and check playback and sentence tracking. Then test recognition on a Kindle book you can access.

Keep in mind

Models require an initial download, and runtime memory exceeds file size. Cloud voices transmit text to providers. Kindle recognition and page turning depend on page structure and may not work with every book.

08BellmanGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 2License MIT

Brief: Sessions Across Providers Join a Shared Room

What happened: Bellman demonstrated cross-session collaboration over MCP. Participants preview a room’s brief and roles before confirming disclosure of their own context. It supports messages, artifacts, action requests, and progress updates, with an A2A entry point as well.

Why it matters: Cross-product collaboration can begin with explicit sharing and role boundaries.

Who should care: Developers coordinating work across model providers.

Try it

Use a test brief to check preview, confirmation, departure, and role restrictions.

Keep in mind

Shared material enters the room. Check hosted-service entitlements and pricing separately.

09EnzymeGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 98License Apache-2.0

Brief: Note Folders Become a Searchable Agent Map

What happened: Enzyme demonstrated local indexing of Markdown folders, tags, links, and dates. Coding agents retrieve related notes and cite original passages. A generated question map helps navigation while existing notes remain unchanged.

Why it matters: Finding decisions across months is more auditable than retaining only a summary.

Who should care: People maintaining long-running notes and project records.

Try it

Test a small public notebook and verify retrieved passages and citations.

Keep in mind

Default setup may use online services. The question map is not evidence.

10ShotstashGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 5License MIT

Brief: Self-Hosted Media Storage Exposes an AI Job Interface

What happened: Shotstash demonstrated self-hosted video storage for creators, with resumable uploads, sharing, and roles. A contract for queues, heartbeats, progress, and outputs connects independently supplied AI workers.

Why it matters: Media storage and processing models can be selected and replaced separately.

Who should care: Small studios with a NAS or server.

Try it

Inspect the read-only demo, then use copies to test upload recovery and job receipts.

Keep in mind

This is not an out-of-the-box AI editor. The reference worker only creates proxy videos.

14Hugging Face speech-to-speechGlobal2026-10-24 published2026-10-25 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 13,547License Apache-2.0

Brief: Speech Framework Breaks Down Turn Latency

What happened: speech-to-speech 1.1.0 adds CPU speech backends, live language selection, and per-turn latency breakdowns for voice detection, recognition, the model, and synthesis. YAML configuration is also new.

Why it matters: Diagnosing response delays no longer depends on one end-to-end number.

Who should care: Teams building real-time voice agents.

Try it

Use Python 3.11 or later and compare time to first text with time to first audio.

Keep in mind

The Phonon checkpoint listed as the default in the v1.1.0 release notes is noncommercial; a permissively licensed alternative is available.

11Amazon SageMaker AIUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-25 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Managed Training Supports Custom Reward Loops

What happened: AWS made SageMaker AI training sessions generally available. Users control sampling, rewards, and update sequencing while the platform manages GPU allocation, recovery, and checkpoints. Billing is based on tokens processed.

Why it matters: Teams can retain training logic without managing clusters or accepting only preset recipes.

Who should care: Teams training models to use tools.

Try it

Run a small cookbook example and track reward-computation and training costs separately.

Keep in mind

Check supported models and regions. Managed GPUs do not make training free.

12EUGEN sparseGlobal2026-10-24 demonstrated2026-10-25 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: A Sparse Training Kernel Separates Speed From Quality

What happened: An independent researcher released Triton code for sparse feed-forward layers, reporting 36% higher training throughput on one RTX PRO 6000. The repository has no weights and does not establish final quality parity with dense training.

Why it matters: Separating speed measurements from quality conclusions makes replication more useful.

Who should care: Developers researching sparse model training.

Try it

Check numerics against the reference implementation, then benchmark your own shapes and GPU.

Keep in mind

CC BY-NC 4.0 prohibits commercial use. Unsupported configurations may take a slow fallback path.

In perspective: The interesting shift is more specific control: a citation, a parameter, a scene, or a claimed task. Inspecting and revising these steps can matter more to everyday reliability than adding a universal shortcut.
