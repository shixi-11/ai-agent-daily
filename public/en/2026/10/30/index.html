The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Evidence

Model Results Get Different Kinds of Checks

Meta exposes behavioral differences with failing tests; Doomformer compares calculations with an integer reference. Both offer inspectable results, but agreement with a reference implementation, classification accuracy, and production benefits are different claims.

Local execution

Local Tools Expose Their Costs

Mistral releases weights, Kinoloom manages GPU memory, and Swift tools offer local decisions. Self-hosting gives users more choice, but also makes them responsible for licensing, memory requirements, and wait times.

Workflow continuity

Speech Tools Connect Writing Workflows

Granola dictates into applications; Overtone reorganizes captions for different screens. Preserving terminology, timing, and reading pace makes the content easier to use in the next stage of a workflow.

01MistralFrance2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Mistral Releases Improved Large 4 Weights

What happened: Mistral released improved Large 4 weights on October 29, moving beyond this month’s API preview to self-hosting. The model has roughly one trillion total parameters and activates about 52 billion per token. It accepts text and images and supports a million-token context and three reasoning levels. Mistral also provides deployment and fine-tuning guidance.

Why it matters: The change is what developers can obtain, not another announcement of the same model. Self-hosting teams can now inspect the weights and requirements, although deployment costs and commercial licensing still shape whether it fits their service.

Who should care: Enterprise and infrastructure teams evaluating self-hosted coding agents and defensive security tools.

Try it

Check frameworks, hardware, and licensing in the model card. Compare all three reasoning levels on your own coding tasks, tracking completion rates alongside token use.

Keep in mind

These are open weights under a custom license, not OSI open source. Commercial model-as-a-service businesses need separate authorization. A specialized cybersecurity variant remains a preview for design partners.

03GranolaUK2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Granola Brings Meeting Vocabulary to Voice Typing

What happened: Granola introduced cross-application voice typing on October 29. Users can dictate into email, Slack, or Claude, with automatic punctuation and paragraph formatting. Granola says the feature is free with unlimited words for its users and can use existing meeting context to recognize colleagues’ names and team terminology. It is available on Mac first.

Why it matters: Context accumulated by a meeting assistant now serves writing between meetings. The benefit is not just speech-to-text: it can reduce name corrections and repeated background explanations in everyday replies.

Who should care: Mac users already using Granola who frequently write emails and long prompts between meetings.

Try it

Dictate a draft with colleagues’ names. Check terminology, punctuation, and paragraphs before sending; automatic cleanup does not verify facts.

Keep in mind

Windows, iOS, and Android are listed as coming soon. The fourfold speed claim assumes specific speaking and typing rates; it is not our measurement. Meeting-context use also depends on team settings.

09IBM GraniteUS2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Granite Updates Adapters, Leaves Base Model Unchanged

What happened: IBM released Granite Switch 4.1.1 on October 29, updating speech-recognition adapters for ten languages and the Decision adapter while leaving the base model unchanged. IBM describes full 4.1 compatibility with no migration.

Why it matters: Modularity lets specialized capabilities change without replacing the base model.

Who should care: Granite users and speech developers.

Try it

Compare punctuation and decision outputs on your own workloads.

Keep in mind

Improvements are vendor-reported. The third-party Adapter SDK is still forthcoming.

10AnthropicUS2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Claude Adds Usage and Cost Analytics APIs

What happened: Anthropic launched its Usage and Cost Analytics API beta on October 29. Two organization-level endpoints report tokens and costs, with grouping by model, workspace, key, or user.

Why it matters: Teams can bring model spending into their own reports rather than relying on a console.

Who should care: Platform teams managing Claude organization budgets.

Try it

Enable the documented beta header and query a short interval first.

Keep in mind

This is a beta. Check organization permissions, available dimensions, and differences from older endpoints.

02MatttGlobal2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 35License Apache-2.0

Swift Turns Model Judgments Into Choices With Probabilities

What happened: Mattt introduced speech and decision packages for Apple platforms on October 29. AnyDecisionModel returns choices, scores, and probabilities without parsing generated prose. Its Emoji Magnet example classifies 343 emoji against a phrase, lifting matching icons from a pile, with local and hosted model options.

Why it matters: The interface connects model judgments to types an application can use. Emoji provide an intuitive demonstration; ticket routing and message classification are more familiar applications. Probabilities also give developers something to evaluate for uncertainty.

Who should care: Swift developers creating Mac apps, local classifiers, or interactive demos.

Try it

Explore Emoji Magnet, then test choices on a small labeled ticket set. The local MLX backend requires Apple silicon; check Swift and operating-system requirements first.

Keep in mind

Concentrated probabilities do not establish correctness. Calibration and separate evaluation remain necessary. The roughly ten-second local emoji pass is the author’s M4 Max measurement; we did not run it.

04DoomformerGlobal2026-10-30 demonstrated2026-10-30 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 10License MIT

DOOM Maps Run a Neural Network

What happened: Doomformer’s creator demonstrated the reverse of an AI playing DOOM: an integer neural network compiled into a game map. A player draws a digit by walking across floor tiles. Voodoo dolls cross trigger lines to compute, while lighting and floor heights store values. The map displays the prediction; a museum map also lets players rewire a connection.

Why it matters: The experiment makes matrix calculations explorable. Players can change a connection and observe the answer, seeing both how computation works and how to check its implementation.

Who should care: Developers and players interested in game modifications, neural-network visualization, and unconventional computing.

Try it

Watch the creator’s videos or open the Colab example, then check the Python and ViZDoom requirements before installing. Use the museum map to compare predictions before and after rewiring.

Keep in mind

EXACT means equality with the integer reference, not correct classification. The author reports roughly 23,000 times slower execution than NumPy for a small network. This is a computing experiment, not an accelerator.

05KinoloomGlobal2026-10-30 demonstrated2026-10-30 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 18License MIT

An 8GB GPU Powers a Local Film Workflow

What happened: Kinoloom’s creator demonstrated a local short-film studio that organizes stories, cast portraits, and shots before rendering with image and video models. It joins clips in latent space and checks continuity. Models take turns using the GPU, with excess weights kept in system RAM. Samples came from a PC with an 8GB RTX 3070.

Why it matters: The project connects cast references, shots, continuity checks, and final assembly into one workflow. It reduces GPU-memory requirements, but users still face long waits and must manage the models.

Who should care: Independent creators and technical enthusiasts willing to configure local video models and inspect an editable workflow.

Try it

Review the samples and shot records, then check Windows, 64GB RAM, and model-download requirements. Start with one short shot rather than a long production.

Keep in mind

Testing covers the author’s single machine. The author estimates that rendering a 16-second sample takes about 28 minutes after warm-up. Seams and drift remain. The MIT code license does not cover model weights. We did not render a video.

07OvertoneGlobal2026-10-30 demonstrated2026-10-30 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 22License MIT

Brief: One Caption File Fits Different Screens

What happened: Overtone’s creator demonstrated a format that stores words, timing, and speaker information once, then reflows captions for 16:9 and 9:16 screens. Existing SRT or WebVTT files can be reformatted offline, with transcription models available as an option.

Why it matters: One piece of content need not inherit its widescreen caption breaks everywhere.

Who should care: Creators and developers publishing video across platforms.

Try it

Start by reflowing existing subtitles, then explore the landscape and portrait player demos.

Keep in mind

Complete rules cover English and Dutch only. Chinese uses phrase-level handling. Translation is not included.

06MetaUS2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Meta Gives Code Review Executable Evidence

What happened: Meta reported production results for JiTTesting on October 29. The system generates failing tests for risky code changes, using execution to expose behavioral differences before model judges and deterministic rules filter the findings. Engineers mainly confirm whether the behavior was intended, rather than reading every generated test.

Why it matters: Code review gains evidence that engineers can run and inspect. Layered filtering saves human attention for meaningful decisions as coding agents take on more work.

Who should care: Engineering leaders responsible for large repositories, automated testing, and AI-assisted code review.

Try it

Read the intent-aware and difference-driven workflows, then test whether generated cases reproduce a known regression. Measure false positives and human review effort as well as detection.

Keep in mind

Meta reports that JiTTesting helped catch 74 serious privacy bugs before production and reduce serious privacy incidents by 20% in the first half of 2026. These are internal privacy results, not a reduction across all bugs. JiTTesting is not available for general download.

08Project ProtoStarUS and global2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: ProtoStar Targets Reusable Research Data

What happened: Hugging Face, universities, and other partners announced ProtoStar on October 29 to share scientific datasets, models, and code in machine-actionable formats. Its first climate challenge opens November 2.

Why it matters: Download access alone is not enough; documentation, metadata, and provenance enable reuse.

Who should care: Research teams and students working in open science.

Try it

Read the inaugural challenge rules for predictions across 210 US cities.

Keep in mind

The challenge has not opened. Final forecasting results require complete 2026 weather records.

11Nua ArtificerGlobal2026-10-30 demonstrated2026-10-30 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: Artificer Estimates Robot Task Progress

What happened: Nua demonstrated Artificer, a 4B-parameter model that estimates completion and progress from robot videos. Such feedback could help controllers continue, retry, or switch tasks. The team invites real-world videos for testing.

Why it matters: Robots need to know what they have accomplished, not just receive another action.

Who should care: Teams researching robot evaluation and task feedback.

Try it

Read how metrics are defined for the two evaluations, then prepare labeled videos for comparison.

Keep in mind

Results are author-reported. We found no public weights or immediately usable product endpoint.

12OpenAIUS2026-10-29 published2026-10-30 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: OpenAI Revises Its Model Spec

What happened: OpenAI updated the Model Spec on October 29, reorganizing it around five values. It adds guidance on proportionate use of context and memory and clarifies how to communicate side effects, obtain authorization, and support human oversight and correction.

Why it matters: As assistants take actions, intent and control boundaries need clearer expression.

Who should care: Teams designing agent products and behavior evaluations.

Try it

Compare the changelog with memory use and authorization prompts for external actions.

Keep in mind

This is a public behavior-specification update, not proof that every deployed model fully implements it.

In perspective: Look beyond stronger models to inspectable deliverables: weights, executable tests, word timings, and explicit requirements. Understanding those details is more useful than assuming a demonstration predicts production results on your own system.
