The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Checking outcomes

Training and Alerts Check External State

ThinkingBox checks final task states; Vercel Agent investigates logs before notifying people. Verification influences rewards and next actions, rather than merely annotating a generated answer.

Human revision

Creative and Research Tools Leave Room to Edit

AgentReel exposes shot matches, while LitPivot lets users revise ideas. The value of automation also depends on whether people can understand and adjust intermediate results.

Access

Local Apps and Games Lower the Entry Barrier

Hob embeds model setup in document work, while Polar Peaks embeds model testing in a game. A concrete activity can explain AI’s usefulness better than an empty chat box.

01OpenAIUS2026-10-26 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Terra Adds Visual Reasoning and Computer Use

What happened: OpenAI has released GPT-6.1 Terra for visual reasoning, computer use, and natural writing. It accepts text and images and produces text, with a 1.05-million-token context window. Standard pricing is $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens. The model is available through the API; tool calls require the Responses API.

Why it matters: Interface automation involves repeated cycles of seeing, acting, and checking. Terra adds another cost option for these workflows, but its value depends on completion rates and retries—not token prices alone.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams building browser assistants, desktop automation, and image-based workflows.

Try it

Compare completion rates, step counts, and total costs on the same interface tasks. Use the Responses API for computer use and other tools.

Keep in mind

This is a closed-weight API model. Inputs above 272,000 tokens incur long-context surcharges, and the model card lists no audio or video input support.

05AgentReelGlobal2026-10-26 demonstrated2026-10-27 observedCreator’s primary source

AgentReel Matches Footage to a Voiceover, Line by Line

What happened: AgentReel’s creator demonstrated a service that matches uploaded footage to a voiceover script and renders captioned landscape or portrait MP4s. Projects support up to 500 clips and 20 GB, with clip names and durations available for review before rendering. The first free trial is limited to 60 seconds and 50 clips, with a user-supplied voiceover recording.

Why it matters: The service targets the time-consuming search through existing footage. For creators with large clip libraries, finding the right shot and showing why it matches can matter more than generating new imagery.

Who should care: Video creators and small teams turning existing footage into narrated short films.

Try it

Try a short narration and a small set of licensed clips. Inspect each line’s shot on the timeline before buying credits for further use.

Keep in mind

We did not test an upload. Missing matches receive fallback clips; 4K footage is compressed to 1080p before upload, and files are retained for seven days.

06Google AI QuestsUS2026-10-26 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Polar Peaks Teaches Students to Check AI Data and Models

What happened: Google Research and the Stanford Accelerator for Learning have launched Polar Peaks, the third AI Quests activity, for students ages 11–14. Players investigate neuron data in a fictional story about memory, completing data-cleaning and model-testing tasks. The activity is available; the teacher-training module for this third quest is due in November 2026.

Why it matters: The lesson puts data quality, model evaluation, and human judgment into the actions of a game. Students encounter a research process, not AI presented as a magic answer machine.

Who should care: Teachers and parents seeking AI-literacy activities, science lessons, and shared learning experiences.

Try it

Open Polar Peaks through the official AI Quests site. Ask learners what data they cleaned, how they evaluated the model, and where human review mattered.

Keep in mind

The story is an educational scenario, not a diagnostic tool for memory problems. The third teacher-training module is not yet available; we did not play through every level.

07IBM GraniteUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Granite Pools Forecasts From Four Models

What happened: IBM released a time-series ensemble recipe that loads four Granite checkpoints at runtime and pools probabilistic forecasts. It is not a new merged model. Across 97 GIFT-Eval configurations, its aggregate mean absolute scaled error (MASE) was 0.683, versus 0.685 for the best of its four constituent models.

Why it matters: The small average gain needs to be weighed against running four models.

Who should care: Researchers and engineers evaluating time-series forecasting.

Try it

Start with the Apache-2.0 recipe and compare error and runtime costs against a single model.

Keep in mind

Channels are handled independently by default. Forecast intervals do not guarantee nominal coverage.

10Vercel AgentUS2026-10-26 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Vercel Agent Investigates Alerts Before Paging

What happened: Vercel Agent can investigate logs and metrics before sending notifications. With Critical Only enabled, a webhook forwards only alerts classified as Critical. The feature is available to Pro and Enterprise customers using Observability Plus.

Why it matters: Classification now affects who gets paged, not just how an alert is summarized.

Who should care: Operations teams using Vercel observability.

Try it

Replay known alerts and compare classifications with the existing notification path.

Keep in mind

Observability Plus includes ten Agent investigations per billing cycle; additional investigations are billed separately. Classification does not guarantee that every incident will be escalated.

12Vercel ConnectUS2026-10-26 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Vercel Connect Makes Over 1,000 Services Searchable by CLI

What happened: Vercel CLI 64.1.0 and later can search more than 1,000 Connect services by name, alias, or description, with filters for MCP, OAuth, and triggers. Results can be returned as JSON before creating a connector.

Why it matters: Service discovery gains a consistent, script-readable entry point.

Who should care: Developers configuring external services for agents.

Try it

Use discover to search for a capability, then inspect service details before connecting.

Keep in mind

Discovering a service does not grant authorization; account permissions still require configuration.

02Microsoft ThinkingBoxUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 3MIT declared in README; GitHub: NOASSERTION

ThinkingBox Turns Final Task States Into Training Rewards

What happened: Microsoft has released ThinkingBox training code that uses final database-state checks as reinforcement-learning rewards. In its experiment, Qwen3.8-27B trained for 50 GRPO steps on 187 private tasks. Mean success rose from 51.70% to 60.78% across a separate 507-task benchmark. This is a new training release, not a relaunch of the benchmark.

Why it matters: The reward asks whether the external state satisfies the task, not whether an answer sounds right. The results also separate consistency from breadth: succeeding more reliably does not necessarily mean solving more kinds of tasks.

Who should care: Reinforcement-learning teams studying tool use, task rewards, and agent reliability.

Try it

Read the MIT-licensed code and public examples, then establish a baseline on tasks with checkable final states. The reference setup uses 24 H100 GPUs.

Keep in mind

The 187 training tasks are not public. Tasks solved at least once in 20 attempts fell from 453 to 446; the higher mean success rate does not imply gains across all tasks.

03HobGlobal2026-10-26 demonstrated2026-10-27 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 98License MIT

Hob: A Local Desktop Document Assistant

What happened: Hob’s creator demonstrated an MIT-licensed desktop assistant that selects an open-weight model to fit the computer and sets it up. The project says it can fill PDFs, work with documents, and run offline. Downloads are listed for Mac ARM64, Windows x64, and Linux. The date marks the creator’s demonstration, not a verified first release.

Why it matters: The barrier to local AI is often choosing a model, setting it up, and working with files—not opening a chat window. Wrapping those steps around document tasks gives everyday users a clearer starting point.

Who should care: People who want local document assistance without manual model setup, and developers building desktop AI tools.

Try it

Choose the appropriate download from Hob’s site or repository and try an ordinary PDF. Check model download size and local resource requirements first.

Keep in mind

Offline operation and privacy are project claims; we did not install the app. Its MIT license does not automatically cover the models it downloads.

04Gottschalk TarantelleGlobal2026-10-26 demonstrated2026-10-27 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 0License CC-BY-SA-4.0

Braille Scores Become Editable Piano Performances

What happened: A creator demonstrated a reconstruction of Gottschalk’s Grande Tarantelle in its solo-piano arrangement. Python decodes Hu Haipeng’s braille transcription, then timing, dynamics, and pedal choices produce MIDI and audio: 603 bars with repeats, about seven and a half minutes. The creator credits Claude; the repository documents the pipeline and outputs.

Why it matters: This is a reconstruction from a specific score, not a prompt for music in a similar style. MIDI leaves room to inspect the rhythm and revise the interpretation rather than simply accept a rendered recording.

Who should care: Creators and developers interested in accessible scores, computational music, and editable performance generation.

Try it

Listen or download the MIDI from the repository, then inspect it in music software that preserves tempo maps. Read the decoding and rendering instructions before reproducing it.

Keep in mind

Code is MIT-licensed; the score and performance outputs use CC BY-SA 4.0. The decoder handles this piece’s notation, not arbitrary braille scores.

08Ai2 and the University of PennsylvaniaUS2026-10-23 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: LitPivot Revises Research Ideas Against the Literature

What happened: Ai2 and the University of Pennsylvania introduced a research prototype that breaks an idea into its problem, proposed contribution, and evaluation plan. LitPivot retrieves papers and grounds suggested revisions in excerpts. Users can accept, edit, or reject those revisions and refresh the literature ranking as their proposal develops.

Why it matters: Iterating between ideas and evidence is easier to inspect than a one-shot topic suggestion.

Who should care: Researchers exploring literature-assisted study design.

Try it

Read the research introduction and examine how proposed revisions connect to cited passages.

Keep in mind

The lab study involved 17 researchers. Their reported gains in novelty do not establish that the revised ideas were scientifically novel.

09Pandora M1Global2026-10-26 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Pandora M1 Puts GPU Monitoring on a Physical Panel

What happened: A creator showed a training-monitor prototype with LED bars and physical knobs for utilization, memory, temperature, and power. It is designed for 64 nodes with eight GPUs each—512 GPUs in total. The circuit board and enclosure remain unfinished.

Why it matters: Training telemetry moves beyond a browser dashboard into a tactile interface.

Who should care: Readers interested in training infrastructure and interactive hardware.

Try it

Watch the creator’s demonstration of metric switching and node selection.

Keep in mind

This is a prototype, with no confirmed retail availability or complete open-hardware release.

11Scale AI and VercelUS2026-10-26 published2026-10-27 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: AgentEnv Separates Agents From Task Environments

What happened: AgentEnv’s new Vercel Sandbox provider runs agents and task environments in separate sandboxes, with actions and scores available for inspection. It requires AgentEnv 0.9.1334 or later and a configured Vercel team and project.

Why it matters: Explicit isolation makes execution and evaluation results easier to inspect separately.

Who should care: Engineering teams maintaining agent evaluation environments.

Try it

Configure the provider using the guide, then inspect actions and scores on a small task.

Keep in mind

The Vercel CLI must be logged in. Account for hosted sandbox costs separately.

In perspective: The common thread is not handing every step to a model, but making important steps easier to check. ThinkingBox’s higher average score comes with narrower task coverage; Granite’s small gain requires more models. Those trade-offs determine whether a capability is worth adopting.
