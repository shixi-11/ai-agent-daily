The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems that make an agent production-ready

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Execution boundaries

Sandboxes must state their limits

Plimsoll reports isolation tiers; Browser Agent Toolkit moves execution into a tab. Neither approach removes the need to inspect network access and backend assumptions.

Verification methods

Different checks answer different questions

BeelineBench measures search detours, while Vizipedia checks quotations and Rustcoach invokes the compiler. A passed check means only what that check was designed to establish.

Learning interfaces

Practice becomes part of the interface

Atlas Runa brings mistakes into later material, while Mira anchors narration to places. Both benefit when users can question the output and adjust what comes next.

04Search Your CollectionGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary source

Search an Image Collection in Plain Language

What happened: The Search Your Collection creator demonstrates a move to EmbeddingGemma 2, encoding text and images in the browser and reordering assets around a natural-language query. The site offers icon, flower, and travel-photo examples. The creator says computation uses WebGPU or WASM, while collections can sync across devices.

Why it matters: Describing the image you have in mind can fit creative work better than remembering filenames. Local computation and local storage are separate: running the model in a browser does not mean the assets stay on the device.

Who should care: Designers and individuals organizing icons, travel photos, brand assets, or visual references.

Try it

Try color, scene, and mood descriptions against the public examples and compare the rankings. Read the synchronization details before deciding whether to upload your own assets and vectors.

Keep in mind

The creator explicitly says files and vectors are stored on the server. The first visit downloads roughly 170 MB of model data, or about 520 MB on capable desktops. Mobile uploads are slow, and generic images may rank highly for unrelated queries.

05Atlas RunaGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary source

Language Practice Brings Mistakes Back into Reading

What happened: Atlas Runa’s creators demonstrate an adaptive language-learning system that uses lookups, saved vocabulary, reviews, writing mistakes, and goals to shape later recommendations. Learners can read graded material, import EPUBs or podcasts, and use new expressions in summaries. The site currently lists Italian, Spanish, Mandarin Chinese, Japanese, and German.

Why it matters: It aims to bridge the gap between recognizing language and producing it, bringing expressions back through reading and writing. The useful test is whether mistakes are remembered and subsequent practice addresses them, not how many recommendations appear.

Who should care: Independent learners and teachers seeking to connect reading with writing practice, particularly once learners have a basic foundation.

Try it

Inspect a word in the site’s sample, then try a summary rewrite and check whether explanations address your own sentence. Set a book or exam goal and see whether later material follows it.

Keep in mind

The product currently runs mainly in a browser; native mobile apps are still planned. A free entry point does not make every service permanently free. Absolute beginners in Chinese or Japanese need to build a foundation first, and model feedback still needs checking.

06MiraGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary source

Mira Adds a Conversational Guide to Street View

What happened: Mira’s creator demonstrates walking audio tours shaped around location and interests. Users can adjust a route together or discover places as they walk. The web experience combines street view with narration: San Francisco and Paris require no registration, while other cities require sign-in. The site offers an initial 30 free minutes and time-based purchases.

Why it matters: The voice assistant has a concrete spatial reference: the building in view and the next stop supply context. Its value depends on accurate narration and walkable routes, not just natural-sounding speech.

Who should care: Readers interested in city walks, travel planning, remote street-view exploration, or voice-product design.

Try it

Try a familiar route in the no-registration street-view demo and check its place names and stories. Before going out, verify the route and opening hours rather than treating generated narration as a guarantee of current conditions.

Keep in mind

The site lists 23 narration languages; Chinese is not currently among them. After the free period, users pay for additional time as needed. We checked the pages and access conditions, not location tracking, route safety, or every historical claim.

10VocafyJapan2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary source

Brief: AI-Assisted Development Makes Originals Easier to Find

What happened: Vocafy’s creator demonstrates a virtual-singer music player built with Claude Code. It finds songs by producer, voicebank, or year, then plays original videos to avoid mixing in covers and reuploads.

Why it matters: A small tool can add value through clear catalog organization without generating more music.

Who should care: Listeners interested in virtual singers and independent music.

Try it

Browse the site by a familiar producer or voicebank.

Keep in mind

The creator says the service is free. iOS background playback and continuous Niconico playback have limitations.

12RustcoachGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary source

Brief: Rust Answers Get Compiler Checks

What happened: Rustcoach’s creator demonstrates a free Rust quiz covering core syntax, async, concurrency, and other topics, saying code answers are compiler-checked. The paid AI course separately integrates tests and review.

Why it matters: Checking executable behavior can constrain explanations based only on a model’s intuition.

Who should care: Learners checking their Rust fundamentals.

Try it

Try the free quiz, then compile any disputed snippet yourself.

Keep in mind

The free quiz does not make the course free. A compiler checks behavior, not teaching effectiveness.

01PlimsollGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary sourceGitHub Stars 1License Apache-2.0

Plimsoll Reports Isolation for Each Run

What happened: Plimsoll’s creator demonstrates a self-hosted code sandbox that reports the actual isolation tier with every result. Requests can require a minimum tier; if it cannot be met, the code does not run. A cart-pole simulation tests an agent-written controller against hidden scenarios, with hashes recorded for requests, results, and trajectories.

Why it matters: It turns “sandboxed” into a more specific, inspectable claim and distinguishes a refusal before execution from a failure after code starts. That distinction matters when deciding whether a retry is safe.

Who should care: Engineering teams running agent-generated code, designing tool permissions, or reviewing execution records.

Try it

Open the repository’s cart-pole report and compare the successful controller with the failed draft. Then read the isolation-tier and credential-broker documentation to establish what your chosen backend actually provides.

Keep in mind

The tier reflects the service’s configuration and startup checks, not hardware-backed remote attestation. Signatures and hashes do not establish task correctness. The cart-pole example is a simulation, not evidence of reliable physical robot control.

03VizipediaGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary sourceGitHub Stars 0License MIT

Vizipedia Lets Agents Edit Interactive Explanations

What happened: Vizipedia’s creator demonstrates an interactive encyclopedia where readers explore a visualization, then ask an agent to submit changes through an API. Pages retain earlier versions, label the model reported by the submitter, and check whether quoted words appear in the cited source. Interactive code runs in a restricted frame. The repository uses MIT licensing.

Why it matters: An AI-authored explanation can be something readers manipulate and compare, not just an answer to read. Version history and source matching make changes easier to trace, but finding a quotation does not establish that the explanation is sound.

Who should care: Teams producing educational pages, course demonstrations, knowledge bases, and interactive documentation.

Try it

Explore a Fourier-transform or sorting demonstration on the public site and inspect its sources and history. Before self-hosting, check runtime requirements, the browser-validation component, and restrictions on interactive code.

Keep in mind

Public reading needs no editing permission; writing requires a key. Model attribution is self-reported. A local installation without the browser component may accept code without a render check, so operators must inspect their configuration.

07Browser Agent ToolkitGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary sourceGitHub Stars 0License not declared

Brief: A Coding Agent Runs Inside a Browser Tab

What happened: Browser Agent Toolkit runs a coding service, filesystem, and dev server in a tab, with hot-reloaded previews after edits. The creator provides a desktop Chrome demo.

Why it matters: Apps can embed a coding environment, but inference costs and network permissions still need separate treatment.

Who should care: Teams building embedded coding tools.

Try it

Inspect the online TODO demo, then read the runtime and network-relay notes.

Keep in mind

The initial load is about 55 MB. Some recordings use scripted model replies, and network allowlisting remains planned.

08TraconGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary sourceGitHub Stars 0License MIT

Brief: A Monitor Watches Parallel Coding Agents

What happened: Tracon’s creator demonstrates a local terminal and browser monitor that reads Claude Code transcripts and worktrees. It flags approval waits, repeated failures, task drift, and file conflicts, sending a nudge to the lead session only after a keypress.

Why it matters: As parallelism grows, detecting stalled work can matter more than adding another agent.

Who should care: Users running several coding agents at once.

Try it

Start with the built-in demo, then inspect conflict alerts in a test worktree.

Keep in mind

The demo is simulated. The optional model judge is enabled by default and sends excerpts out; it can be disabled.

02BeelineBenchGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary sourceGitHub Stars 1License Apache-2.0

BeelineBench Measures Detours in Model Planning

What happened: BeelineBench’s creator demonstrates an evaluation that puts decision models inside best-first search to rank states awaiting exploration. Seven domains include sliding puzzles, block planning, and Wikipedia navigation. Scores compare the shortest path with the number of nodes explored, alongside classic heuristics, random choice, and an oracle baseline.

Why it matters: A correct individual choice does not establish efficient sequential decisions. The benchmark exposes the cost of detours and explicitly warns that classic search often suits these tasks better. Model scores are not proof that conventional algorithms should be replaced.

Who should care: Developers and researchers studying decision APIs, multistep planning, tool routing, and evaluation methods.

Try it

Run the heuristic and oracle baselines without model calls first. Then fix the random seed for comparisons and read scores alongside node budgets, frontier limits, and API costs.

Keep in mind

The published results are the author’s experiments, not independently reproduced here. Runs stop at 2,500 explored nodes; treating unsolved runs at that cap makes their displayed scores optimistic. The frontier limit also affects results.

09TilefinchGlobal2026-10-11 demonstrated / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary sourceGitHub Stars 38License MIT

Brief: An Old PSP Tries Modern Pages and Games

What happened: Tilefinch’s creator demonstrates a browser for the 333 MHz, 64 MB PSP that handles pages and scripts on the handheld. Its WebGL tank game explores what agent-assisted development can achieve.

Why it matters: Constrained hardware brings software size and execution costs back into view.

Who should care: Retro-hardware and lightweight-runtime enthusiasts.

Try it

Check the repository’s device screenshots, compatibility list, and installation requirements.

Keep in mind

This is experimental: some sites are slow or incomplete. It does not mean a large language model runs on the PSP.

11CohereCanada2026-10-06 published / 2026-10-12 observedPrimary source

Brief: Multilingual Reasoning Depends on the Data Mix

What happened: An October 6 Cohere article examines mixing English reasoning, multilingual reasoning, and multilingual question-answer data. It reports that over 93% of reasoning traces across 60 languages use the prompt language, while disclosing a decline on difficult math tasks.

Why it matters: Answering in a language and showing reasoning in that language are different capabilities.

Who should care: Multilingual-product and model-training teams.

Try it

Read the three-way data ablations and inspect low-resource language results separately.

Keep in mind

This is a research analysis, not a new weight release. Language consistency does not establish correct or faithful reasoning.

In perspective: A useful AI tool makes its outputs easier to explore without hiding the conditions behind them. Check where data goes, where actions execute, and what a successful test actually proves.
