The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Creative control

Creative Tools Refine Human-Agent Roles

Recto enables direct canvas edits; Pika uses references and timed instructions to direct video and sound. Concrete revision controls help creators retain authority over details.

Identity and action

Enterprise Agents Must Carry User Permissions

AgentCore propagates identity downstream; SafeWebWorld shows that plausible predictions can still lead to flawed authorization. Tool access and action rehearsal cannot replace permission checks.

Runtime structure

Infrastructure Separates Releases From Execution

Meta separates release boundaries from shared computation, while TensorRT-LLM reuses caches across turns. Efficiency depends on reusable state and isolated changes, not model speed alone.

01Atria DawnChina2026-10-23 weights uploaded UTC2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

Atria Dawn Releases Full Research Model Weights

What happened: Shanghai AI Laboratory published full Atria Dawn weights, built on the 744B-parameter GLM-5.2 mixture-of-experts model. It supports a 512K context and documents SGLang and vLLM deployment. The model targets multi-step research, coding, experiments, and deliverables. This is coverage of the full weights uploaded October 23, not a launch today.

Why it matters: Research assistants increasingly compete on turning questions into experiments and results. Full weights let teams inspect serving interfaces and tool workflows, but reported end-to-end scores still require matched environments and budgets.

Who should care: Teams deploying large models for scientific automation and long-context tool use.

Try it

Read the model card and serving configuration, compare the full release with Preview, and assess cluster capacity. Start with a small experiment that has explicit acceptance criteria.

Keep in mind

The model page declares MIT; check base-model and dependency terms separately. This is not a lightweight single-machine model. Publisher benchmarks were not independently tested here; the date refers to the weights upload.

02Pika H3 FlexGlobal2026-10-29 weights uploaded UTC2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

Pika Uses Reference Adapters to Direct Video and Sound

What happened: Pika H3 Flex publishes adapters and examples built on MiniMax-H3, demonstrating six-step synchronized audio-video generation with up to twelve reference inputs. Its long example prompts specify camera moves, actions, dialogue, and sound, using reference material to constrain characters and scenes.

Why it matters: Video control is moving from describing a look toward directing what happens over time. Downloadable adapters let researchers inspect reference conditioning and audio-video prompts, though elaborate examples do not prove reliable compliance on every generation.

Who should care: Creators and model developers exploring multi-reference video, synchronized audio, and camera control.

Try it

Inspect the table-tennis camera sequence and sitcom samples, then compare reference formats and six-step settings. Use authorized short materials to assess action and sound alignment.

Keep in mind

Weights use the MiniMax-H3 community license; example code is Apache-2.0. Do not call the entire model Apache open source. Examples use an H100 80GB, not a demonstrated consumer-GPU setup. No video was generated here.

03RectoGlobal2026-11-03 demonstrated2026-11-04 observedCreator’s primary source

Recto Gives People and Coding Agents a Shared Canvas

What happened: Recto showcased a local visual workspace that connects installed coding tools such as Claude Code and Codex. Users can move selected interface elements directly or send contextual comments to an agent. The site demonstrates UI, SVG, motion, and 3D workspaces and offers a free desktop download.

Why it matters: Design collaboration often stalls over identifying exactly what should change. Object selection, direct edits, and prompts in one workspace reduce screenshot-based explanations while keeping people in control of design decisions.

Who should care: Designers and independent developers using coding assistants for interfaces, motion, and interactive prototypes.

Try it

Download the desktop app, connect an existing CLI, and compare direct dragging, selection-based prompts, and canvas comments on a simple interface. Check supported systems first.

Keep in mind

The date is the author’s public showcase, not a verified first launch. A local workspace does not imply offline model inference; accounts and model charges are separate. It was not installed or tested here.

What happened: Cohere added managed serving for third-party models including DeepSeek, GLM, and Gemma. Standard Model Vault serves the US, EU, and Canada. Model Vault Encrypted is currently US-only and supports a smaller model set.

Why it matters: Model choice, serving region, and confidential computing are becoming separate deployment decisions.

Keep in mind

This is not a new Cohere foundation model. Do not apply standard-tier model or regional coverage to the encrypted tier.

07TensorRT-LLMUS2026-10-28 published2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 15,002License not declared

TensorRT-LLM Pre-Release Extends Cross-Turn Cache Reuse

What happened: NVIDIA’s TensorRT-LLM 1.4.0rc2 adds native retention and cross-turn generation-block reuse in KV cache V2, alongside support for Cosmos3-Edge, GLM-5.2, and MiniMax-M3. Its release page specifies Python wheels as 1.4.0rc2.post1, while containers and documentation remain at rc2.

Why it matters: Multi-turn agents need to avoid reprocessing history, not just generate faster. Cache-lifecycle and model-support changes should be evaluated against real request patterns and hardware rather than a universal speedup claim.

Who should care: Infrastructure teams running inference on NVIDIA GPUs, supporting multi-turn tool calls, or using disaggregated serving.

Try it

Check breaking API changes in a test environment, then compare cache hits, time to first token, and memory for a fixed multi-turn workload. Select the documented wheel or container version.

Keep in mind

This covers an October 28 pre-release, not a stable version. The release notes warn of initialization hangs when an MPI worker pool is reused across threads. Some features are hardware-specific; performance was not tested here.

11AWS AgentCore GatewayUS2026-11-03 published2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

AgentCore Carries User Identity Into ServiceNow

What happened: AWS published an on-behalf-of token-exchange tutorial for AgentCore Gateway. An agent presents a user token; Microsoft Entra ID exchanges it for a ServiceNow-scoped token so ServiceNow applies the original user’s permissions. The exchange is configured on the gateway target and runs per request, without custom exchange code in the application.

Why it matters: An API connection does not ensure that downstream calls retain user-level permissions. This example separates application identity from user identity, allowing downstream controls and audit records to identify the person initiating the request.

Who should care: Teams connecting enterprise ticketing systems, cross-cloud identity, and agent authorization chains.

Try it

Follow the tutorial in a developer instance. Test users with different permissions and verify the downstream user mapping, not just whether a request succeeds.

Keep in mind

This is a configuration tutorial, not automatic authorization for every API. It requires an MCP 2025-11-25 gateway, correct scopes, and consent. Cloud charges apply; it was not deployed here.

12Meta F3 LightningUS2026-11-03 published2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

Meta Separates Feature Releases From Shared Execution

What happened: Meta described F3 Lightning, which separates the release unit for ads ML features from their execution graph. Features can be validated, deployed, and rolled back independently. Startup merges duplicate work; a shared buffer and dense indexes reduce request-time overhead. Meta reports roughly 3.6 times faster delivery for comparable extraction features, without higher infrastructure compute cost.

Why it matters: Independent deployment often trades release speed for duplicated work and memory. This design keeps release isolation while recovering execution reuse. Deployment boundaries need not match computation boundaries, and counting operations alone can miss memory costs.

Who should care: Infrastructure teams maintaining online feature platforms, high-throughput serving, and shared execution graphs.

Try it

Read the four optimizations: stable computation identifiers, a merged execution plan, a shared buffer, and dense indexes. Look for invariant request work that can move to startup.

Keep in mind

The 3.6-times figure concerns delivery to production, not inference speed. This is an account of Meta’s internal system, not an open-source release. Cost claims were not independently reproduced here.

13Agent Harness SpecificationGlobal2026-10-29 published2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Record the Harness Alongside Agent Scores

What happened: A paper decomposes the agent harness into eight functional components and proposes portable, versioned records of which model ran with which tools, configuration, and infrastructure, including changes during a run.

Why it matters: The same model can score very differently across harnesses; its name alone is not a reproducible setup.

Who should care: Teams comparing agent evaluations and maintaining execution environments.

Try it

Read the eight-component definition and compare it with your run records.

Keep in mind

This is a specification proposal, not an adopted industry standard or a claim that law mandates this common record.

14SafeWebWorldGlobal2026-10-29 published2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Plausible Predictions Do Not Establish Permission

What happened: SafeWebWorld researchers evaluated 3,000 web predictions from ten frozen open models. Humans judged 86.1% semantically plausible, yet distinguishing EXECUTE, ASK, and BLOCK remained difficult. Preserving evidence did not ensure better authorization decisions.

Why it matters: A convincing predicted future is not a substitute for deciding whether an action is authorized.

Who should care: Teams designing browser agents and pre-action evaluations.

Try it

Compare results separately for EXECUTE, ASK, and BLOCK decisions.

Keep in mind

This is a specific web benchmark. The plausibility percentage is not an overall agent safety rate.

15Agentic World ModelingGlobal2026-10-29 published2026-11-04 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Rehearse Agent Actions Through Read-Only Queries

What happened: Agentic World Modeling uses multiple read-only queries to the real environment, then executable computation to construct the next observation for a hypothetical action. Across three benchmarks, the paper explores parallel trajectory search without replicating the environment or mutating shared state.

Why it matters: Exploring parallel paths need not mean writing to the same business system in parallel.

Who should care: Developers exploring planning and test-time scaling in shared environments.

Try it

Read the read-only interface constraints and trajectory-selection process, then inspect tool side effects.

Keep in mind

Preprint benchmark results do not establish zero side effects in production. Experiments were not reproduced here.
