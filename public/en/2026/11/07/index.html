The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Input interfaces

Sage and Inkling Connect Different Inputs

Sage projects scientific features into language-model inputs; Inkling uses image feedback to revise creations. Input representation and base-model capability need separate checks.

Participation

Ampersand and Spaces Change Interaction

Ampersand can choose to wait, while Spaces keeps an idea available as an interface. Appearing at the right moment is becoming part of an assistant's utility.

Deployment boundaries

MIG and XAA Define Sharing Boundaries

MIG partitions GPU resources; XAA distinguishes agent delegation from user authority. Shared infrastructure still needs independent constraints for each participant.

01Inkling 2 SmallUS2026-11-05 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

Inkling 2 Small Reaches Tinker

What happened: Thinking Machines introduces Inkling 2 Small, a mixture-of-experts model with 594 billion total and 17 billion active parameters. It supports image input and variable reasoning effort. Inference, fine-tuning, and a playground are available on Tinker. Weights remain closed; the lab plans an Apache 2.0 release next week after external safety testing.

Why it matters: The model combines visual feedback, long-horizon coding, and customization. Developers can explore iterative websites and 3D work while retaining a path to further training. API access, fine-tuning, and downloadable weights are separate availability claims.

Who should care: Developers and researchers exploring multimodal coding, extended research, and post-training.

Try it

Try a small visual revision in Tinker Playground and check whether successive feedback is incorporated. Read the conditions for fine-tuning and checkpoint downloads rather than extrapolating from showcase examples.

Keep in mind

Audio is unsupported. The lab acknowledges verbosity and imperfect tool selection. Weight and checkpoint downloads await release. Benchmark results are vendor-reported and were not independently reproduced here.

02Sage-SciPerceptionChina2026-11-06 weights uploaded UTC2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

InternLM Releases Nine Scientific Perception Projectors

What happened: InternLM releases nine GLM projectors in Sage-SciPerception for tasks involving crystals, spectra, proteins, and biological signals. The components map scientific features into a language model's input space. Weights, configurations, and a vLLM crystal-inference example are downloadable. These components connect to an existing language model; they do not supply a complete new base model.

Why it matters: Scientific data need not be forced into text first. A specialized encoder supplies features, a projector connects them to a language model, and the model generates an answer. Modular artifacts allow input formats, revisions, and outputs to be checked separately; task-specific scores are not an overall capability ranking.

Who should care: Scientists and developers building multimodal tools for materials, biological sequences, and research inference.

Try it

Download a crystal projector and inspect its dimensions on CPU. Match the configured base-model revision and feature format, then deploy the language-model service separately.

Keep in mind

No license is specified for this release, so it should not be described as OSI-approved open source. Scientific encoders and preprocessing are separate dependencies; task-adapted models for DNA, MPBind, and ncRNA are not included. Inference was not run here.

03Meta Muse SpacesUS2026-11-06 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

Muse Turns Ideas Into Persistent Mini Apps

What happened: Meta introduces Muse Spaces: describe a gift tracker, daily quiz, or trip planner to create an interactive space inside the app, then revise its appearance and behavior. Place spaces in the sidebar or main tabs, hide them, or schedule their appearance. Connected apps can keep their content updated in the background.

Why it matters: An assistant’s interface can organize tools around a personal routine rather than a fixed chat box. Persistent state and direct editing make this more useful than a static dashboard. Users still need to check the permissions and sources behind background updates.

Who should care: Muse users organizing plans, interests, and small work projects, and designers of personal assistants.

Try it

Start with a quiz or trip draft containing no private information. Change a rule, leave, and return. Check placement and scheduled visibility as well as whether your edits persist.

Keep in mind

Available wherever Muse is offered, not necessarily in every country. Sharing remains under exploration; collaborative spaces have not been announced as available. Background updates depend on connected apps.

04Humans& AmpersandUS2026-11-06 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

Ampersand Can Speak or Wait in Group Chats

What happened: Ampersand from Humans& is now on Vercel AI Gateway, free through November 13. Built for group conversations, it can choose to contribute or wait on each turn. The application supplies send_message and pass tools, executes them, and records results rather than posting every model response to the chat.

Why it matters: Knowing when not to interrupt is part of collaboration. An explicit waiting action lets products manage shared goals and conversational timing. The application still owns access checks, durable history, and delivery deduplication.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams building group assistants and collaborative agents.

Try it

Try the official playground: invite a clarifying question, then ask the model to wait for another person. With the API, retain receipts and verify that pass does not start another model turn.

Keep in mind

This is a gateway availability update, not a claim of the model's first release. Tool choice supports auto and none, not forced or named tools. Free access has an end date; delivery and retries remain application responsibilities.

05ldraw-mechaGlobal2026-11-06 demonstrated2026-11-07 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 10License AGPL-3.0

ldraw-mecha Infers LEGO Mechanisms Before Animating Them

What happened: The creator demonstrates ldraw-mecha v0.5.0: an agent reads annotated LDraw source, infers submodels and mechanisms, plans motion, and generates Blender rigging and animation scripts. It animates existing LEGO models rather than inferring motion from a generated picture.

Why it matters: Parts, inferred relationships, and motion plans provide inspectable intermediate steps. Crane and aircraft examples also expose the limits: complex mechanisms may need a narrower scope and human judgment. A successful animation is not general physical validation.

Who should care: LEGO mechanism enthusiasts, Blender animators, and developers exploring code-driven creation.

Try it

Read a small example in the gallery and PROCESS guide, comparing source, inference, and animation plans. Installation needs Python 3.12 or later and downloads dependencies including Blender and LeoCAD.

Keep in mind

Linux and Windows/WSL support remain untested work in progress. The complex truck example animates only its crane. The date marks the creator's showcase, not a verified first release. Animation was not run here.

13pico-WAMUS2026-11-06 demonstrated2026-11-07 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 12License MIT

Brief: Dissecting Robot Planning Failures

What happened: pico-WAM separates action proposals from trajectory scoring in Push-T experiments. Code and reproduction instructions accompany a study using 206 demonstrations and 15 training seeds. The study covers this small environment, not general-purpose robotics.

Why it matters: Separate interventions help locate whether proposals or scoring limit planning.

Who should care: Developers studying world models and robot planning.

Global Technology and Market Watch

06SageMaker MIGUS2026-11-06 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

SageMaker Splits GPUs Into Isolated Model Partitions

What happened: AWS adds GPU partitioning to SageMaker endpoints that use inference components. NVIDIA MIG divides a physical GPU into independent compute and memory partitions, each able to host a different model. Configure a layout and choose each component's profile instead of reserving an entire accelerator per model.

Why it matters: Smaller units can better fit tenant models, rerankers, and safety classifiers. Capacity planning still needs to account for weights, KV cache, and runtime overhead, not just whether a model loads.

Who should care: Infrastructure teams serving multiple small or midsize models and managing inference cost and GPU isolation.

Try it

Check profiles for the chosen instance. Compare whole GPUs and partitions under realistic input lengths and concurrency, recording first-token latency, failures, and cost per million output tokens.

Keep in mind

Single-model and serverless endpoints and heterogeneous instance pools are unsupported. Changing the layout requires a new endpoint and traffic migration. GPU isolation excludes host CPU and networking; cost improvements come from specific AWS tests.

07Okta and AgentCoreUS2026-11-06 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

AgentCore Moves Delegated Tool Checks to the Gateway

What happened: An AWS and Okta tutorial moves token exchange from the agent to AgentCore Gateway. Default-deny Cedar policies check delegated tool access before execution. A second agent on EKS has its own identity, demonstrating that the same user can receive different tool access through different agents.

Why it matters: A user’s authority need not transfer wholesale to every agent acting for them. Separate checks for agent identity, delegated scope, and user groups reduce duplicated policy and reject unauthorized calls before tools run.

Who should care: Platform and security teams connecting multiple agents to enterprise applications and centralized authorization.

Try it

Read both parts of the identity flow. Test missing groups, wrong agent identity, excess scope, and valid delegation. Check tool-list pagination and retain server-side user authorization.

Keep in mind

Each agent still needs its own gateway and credential provider. Gateway policy does not replace user-group checks in MCP servers. The snippets illustrate a pattern, not a complete production-ready deployment.

14BlockLockGlobal2026-11-04 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Fixed Batch Geometry Protects Inference Reproducibility

What happened: BlockLock fixes the computational geometry seen by a protected request while retaining shared batched prefill. Its two modes preserve target sequences and reported logit bits as companion requests change in specified model and GPU deployments.

Why it matters: Reproducibility depends on the numerical path through batching, not just random seeds.

Who should care: Teams studying deterministic inference and regression testing.

Try it

Read the per-deployment conditions, then vary companion requests while checking the protected target.

Keep in mind

These results depend on the stated deployment conditions and tests; they do not guarantee identical output across hardware.

15Two-Ledger ModelGlobal2026-11-04 published2026-11-07 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Reconcile Agent Calls With the Financial Bill

What happened: The Two-Ledger Model pairs a provider settlement ledger with invocation-level attribution to orders or cases. A correlation chain and versioned rate card connect them. The author simulates broken tracing and unmetered spend using synthetic data.

Why it matters: A monthly provider bill reveals spending, but not necessarily which business transaction incurred it.

Who should care: Teams managing agent cost attribution and FinOps.

Try it

Read the attribution fields and reconciliation residual, then check a small de-identified dataset for gaps.

Keep in mind

Coverage and cost improvements come from simulation, not measured enterprise production savings.
