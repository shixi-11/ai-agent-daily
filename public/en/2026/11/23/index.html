RISC Machine Primer

RISC = the four systems that make an agent production-ready

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience — BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, one failure can erase progress.
I | Intelligence — BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, uses tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security — Immune SystemObject-capability security, policy-based approvals, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius.
C | Connectivity — Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to one product.

01AWS QuickUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-21 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Quick Brings Multi-Agent Coordination Into Team Workflows

What changed: AWS updated Quick with conversational flow building, skills, agents, and deterministic calculations. Its web app adds briefings and Mission Control, while long-running work continues after the browser closes. Specialized agents can work in parallel and combine answers with contributions identified.

Why it matters: Knowing how to use AI individually is different from building a process a team can reuse. Visible steps and fixed calculations make recurring work easier to inspect, but completion reports still need to match actual documents and business outcomes.

Who should care: Business and platform teams automating reports, research, and team queries.

Try it

Try an existing monthly-report checklist on test data. Check formulas, agent contributions, and the resulting draft before sharing the flow.

Keep in mind

AWS says the features are available in existing Quick Regions. Prompt guardrails remain in preview, and some controls require Professional or Enterprise plans. The finance scenario is fictional; we have not tested the release.

02Amazon Textract InsightsUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Textract Gives Extracted Values Traceable Sources

What changed: Amazon Textract Insights is generally available. Users describe fields using JSON Schema and natural language, then submit a mixed document packet to an asynchronous API. The service splits documents, matches blueprints, and returns confidence scores and page-level coordinates for both explicit and inferred fields. A single file can contain up to 3,000 pages.

Why it matters: Reviewers need to trace a value, not just read a fluent answer. Separating extraction from inference creates a review path, while confidence still needs calibration on the team’s own documents.

Who should care: Engineering and review teams processing financial, insurance, healthcare, and mixed forms.

Try it

Build a blueprint for public sample forms. Verify each field’s page and coordinates, and measure errors separately for direct extraction, cross-document inference, and human review.

Keep in mind

This is a managed API, not an open model. We have not tested accuracy. Core’s 40-language coverage does not establish identical Insights support; Insights is not yet available in Tokyo or Zurich.

03Gemini TasksUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-17 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Gemini Connects Everyday Tasks to Account Context

What changed: Google is rolling out free Gemini task capabilities in the US, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia, and India using Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Users can switch to task mode in the app and hand off work through text messages or email. Advanced features start in the US, including appointment calls, bill analysis, and shopping handoffs.

Why it matters: Personal assistants increasingly depend on authorized access to calendars, email, and services. Account context reduces manual handoffs. Appointments, receipts, and downstream state establish completion; conversational promises do not.

Who should care: Product teams building personal agents, connected applications, and service handoffs.

Try it

Check regional and account eligibility, then try a small task without payment. Review the calendar entry, confirmation steps, and how to disconnect apps.

Keep in mind

Features roll out by region; calls and payments are not universally available. Google describes isolated virtual machines and confirmation for critical steps. Those controls are not a risk-free guarantee, and we have not tested them.

Brief: Ask Maps Connects Itineraries to Live Places

What changed: Ask Maps adds day-by-day itineraries, saved-list search, and appointment discovery. Plans can be shared or exported to Google Docs. Route tips and appointment finding launch first in the US; other capabilities follow Ask Maps coverage.

Why it matters: Connecting plans to locations and available slots reduces repeated page switching.

10WeMM-Video-35B-A3B GGUFChinaPublished 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

WeMM Adds a Larger MoE Bundle for Local Inference

What changed: Tencent uploaded a GGUF Q4_K_M bundle for WeMM-Video-35B-A3B on November 20, adding a larger variant to the smaller models previously covered. Its language component is 22.61 GiB, with separate vision and audio components. The native runtime accepts text, images, audio, and video with its original soundtrack, returning text.

Why it matters: Fewer active parameters do not imply small deployment files. Pinning matching language, vision, and audio components is essential when evaluating a local audiovisual pipeline.

Who should care: Teams evaluating bilingual audiovisual understanding and local multimodal quantization.

Try it

Read the native-runtime guide, pin one Hub revision, and download its three matching components. Compare video-only input with video plus original audio, then inspect outputs and resource use.

Keep in mind

This is a catch-up on a November 20 variant, not a launch today. Check Apache-2.0 and third-party terms separately. File sizes are not peak memory requirements; we have not built or tested it.

15Inkling 2 MiniUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Brief: Inkling Mini Reuses Layers to Reduce Stored Weights

What changed: Thinking Machines Lab released open weights for Inkling 2 Mini, which accepts images and text and generates text with up to one million tokens of context. It has 26.4B physical parameters and 3.4B active parameters per token; its middle layers run three times.

Why it matters: Repeated computation reuses stored weights rather than simply reducing layer count.

Who should care: Developers of local multimodal tools and coding agents.

Try it

Read the model card and choose a documented inference backend for your hardware.

Keep in mind

Apache-2.0 applies. Looped execution gives 5.4B effective active parameters per token. We have not tested it; checkpoint size is not runtime memory.

16Hy4 flashChinaPublished 2026-11-16 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Brief: TokenHub Adds the Hy4 Flash Model

What changed: Tencent TokenHub added Hy4 flash on November 16, separately from the Hy4 release on November 4. Its official model list specifies a one-million-token context, image understanding, tool calling, and retained reasoning.

Why it matters: Flash adds another model choice in the series, not a repeat of the earlier Hy4 launch.

Who should care: Developers integrating Tencent-hosted multimodal models.

Try it

Check the hy4-flash model identifier, input and output limits, and pricing.

Keep in mind

The documented maximum input is 960k tokens and maximum output is 64k. We have not tested speed, quality, or cost.

04Vercel BoardUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-18 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary sourceGitHub Stars 194License Apache-2.0

Board Gives Different Agents Shared Task State

What changed: Vercel introduced Board as an experimental coordination primitive. Channels, threads, and labels store tasks, discussions, and results. Its CLI and JavaScript SDK support claims, lease renewals, and completion reports. Events can wake agents and support two-way synchronization with Slack and GitHub. Structured messages can also be validated against custom schemas.

Why it matters: Agents need more than shared chat: they need ownership, valid leases, and links to results. Common state helps handoffs but does not prove that the work itself is complete.

Who should care: Developers coordinating coding assistants, agent fleets, and work with people.

Try it

Use a separate test board for two tasks. Before synchronizing tools, check lease expiry, duplicate claims, and links to results.

Keep in mind

The repository uses Apache-2.0, and the product is experimental. Part of the website demonstration uses scripted events with no models running. It is not evidence of agent success rates; we have not deployed it.

05Distributed KV Caching SkillUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-21 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary sourceParent repository GitHub Stars 3,542License Apache-2.0

A Caching Skill Checks Costs Before Configuring Shared KV Storage

What changed: AWS published a distributed KV caching skill for coding agents. It checks GPU and inference-engine requirements, uses KVTrace to estimate repeated context and capacity needs, and guides ElastiCache integration. It requires consistent KV identifiers and data types, then checks that two shared-cache loads produce matching outputs after clearing local cache tiers.

Why it matters: Healthy cache-hit counters can coexist with wrong answers when layouts differ. Fit checks, cost estimates, and output validation are more useful than copying a headline savings percentage.

Who should care: Inference engineers serving long contexts through self-hosted vLLM or SGLang.

Try it

Read the skill and KVTrace workflow. Compare recomputation with retrieval on sanitized traffic, then verify model, tensor parallelism, and KV data types before testing.

Keep in mind

The workflow requires GPU instances that support EFA RDMA writes, along with specified engine and cache versions. Peak savings are conditional vendor benchmarks, not our tests. Cache hashes must still be handled as sensitive data.

Global Technology and Market Watch

06Meta Technique PropagationUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-18 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Meta Uses Agents to Transfer Proven ML Techniques

What changed: Meta described an ads-ranking system in which agents find adaptation candidates, generalize proven techniques, manage multiday training, and prepare deployment proposals. Shared templates handle deterministic propagation; agents make judgments and diagnose failures. Changes still need offline gains, cost guardrails, and online A/B wins.

Why it matters: The unit of automation is transferring a useful technique across models with different constraints, not merely generating code. Shared experiment knowledge and stage-specific checks help scale long-running work while preserving human decisions.

Who should care: Teams managing model portfolios, training platforms, and long-running research agents.

Try it

Read the discovery, generalization, and adoption metrics. Map one proven optimization’s adaptation requirements and stopping rules before comparing it with a manual workflow.

Keep in mind

This is an internal Meta case study, not a complete downloadable platform. Meta reports that each engineer can handle three times as many technique-model adaptations and that median training compute fell to one-quarter across selected combinations. These results do not apply to all training workloads.

09HyperPod Global Inference GatewayUnited StatesPublished 2026-11-20 / Observed 2026-11-23Primary source

Brief: HyperPod Adds a Cross-Region Inference Entry Point

What changed: AWS introduced Global Inference Gateway to route among registered HyperPod clusters using signals including cache headroom, queue depth, and Region. It moves traffic away from failed clusters while preserving existing in-cluster pod routing.

Why it matters: A single routing layer can direct requests to spare capacity across regions.

Who should care: Multicluster inference and cloud-platform teams.

Try it

Read the four routing weights and simulate cluster withdrawal and recovery.

Keep in mind

Failed requests are not retried after streaming begins. With no healthy cluster, the gateway returns 503.

This issue’s takeaway: Check task ownership and trace results to their inputs. Verify what actually changed before deciding what to delegate next.

Brief: Meta Releases a Faster Version of Its Atomic Model

What changed: Meta announced UMA Nitro at its Open Source AI for Science Summit, describing it as a faster version of the Universal Model for Atoms. The event also highlighted UMA work on crystal structures and enzymes, plus interactive tools for chemistry classes.

Why it matters: Atomic-scale models are reaching both scientific computing and education.

Who should care: Teams in computational chemistry, materials science, and science education.

Try it

Read the official calculator documentation for Nitro mode and check its fit for your task.

November 20 is the date of Meta’s summit recap, not a verified first-release date; the summit took place the previous month. The speed claim comes from Meta, not our tests. The research results discussed at the event are not all attributable to Nitro alone.
