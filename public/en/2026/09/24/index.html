RISC Machine Guide

RISC = four systems for a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep working, withstand failures, prevent overreach, and collaborate across real organizations.

The industry has delivered a capable brain. Production agents also need a body, an immune system, and a social interface.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scaling. One failure should not erase the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration determine how an agent reasons and completes complex work.
S | Security / ImmunityObject capabilities, policy approvals, rollback, and audit limit the reach of failures and unauthorized actions.
C | Connectivity / SocietyCross-company authorization, a neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connections let agents work beyond one product.

01FLUX 3 ActionEuropePublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

FLUX turns video prediction into robot actions

What changed: Black Forest Labs introduced FLUX 3 Action on September 23. The 7B-parameter world action model predicts future frames and actions together, returning 32 actions per call. DROID and SO-101 weights, training code, and LeRobot integration are public. The team also fine-tuned it for a shooter, a racing game, and simulated indoor drone control.

Why it matters: Visual generation is becoming a foundation for control policies. Weights, recipes, and execution interfaces make the work more reproducible than a demonstration alone.

Who should care: Teams researching robot policies, world models, and game agents using public implementations.

Try it

Read the SO-101 and DROID cards, then choose the matching LeRobot setup. Public game recipes offer another way to examine action prediction and replanning.

Watch for

Code uses Apache-2.0; weights use the custom FLUX Kommunity license, not an OSI-approved open-source license. Results apply to specific tasks, not universal robot performance.

02Nemotron 3 DiarizationUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Nemotron tracks turns across eight speakers

What changed: NVIDIA introduced Nemotron 3 Diarization on September 23. The open-weight 100M-parameter model supports up to eight speakers, overlapping speech, and streaming. It outputs activity labels and timestamps for anonymous speakers, which can be combined with automatic speech recognition (ASR). An online demo offers prepared conversations, live microphone input, and file uploads.

Why it matters: Accurate words can still be assigned to the wrong person. Separating speaker attribution from transcription helps teams evaluate meeting summaries and voice-agent memory more precisely.

Who should care: Teams building meeting records, group voice assistants, interview search, and live audio analytics.

Try it

Try a prepared conversation in the official Space and compare interruptions with its timeline. Evaluate both transcription and speaker attribution when integrating it.

Watch for

The model neither identifies people by name nor transcribes speech on its own. The lowest recommended setting uses a 0.32-second input buffer, not a 0.32-second end-to-end response time. Networking, inference, and speech recognition add to the delay.

03LensVLM-9BUnited StatesWeights published 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Apple’s LensVLM scans page previews before opening full text

What changed: Apple published LensVLM-9B weights on September 23 Beijing time. The accompanying code renders text as compressed page images, then uses learned tool calls to expand only relevant pages into full text. It includes 5x, 10x, and 15x compression settings, data preparation, demos, and evaluation scripts. The paper predates this release.

Why it matters: Long-context reading need not load every page in full. Combining visual overviews with selective evidence retrieval raises a useful question: what gets missed as compression increases?

Who should care: Developers studying document QA, visual text compression, retrieval policies, and tool-using models.

Try it

Start with the official multipage QA demo. Check whether selected pages contain the evidence, then compare compression levels and missed details on your own documents.

Watch for

This is a weights release, not a new paper announcement. Model files use the Apple Machine Learning Research Model License; code uses the separate Apple Sample Code License.

04Gemini Connected AppsUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Gemini connects more creative and everyday apps

What changed: Google began rolling out a new group of Connected Apps for Gemini on September 23. Productivity additions include Airtable, Linear, and Wispr AI; creative tools include Adobe, Picsart, Squarespace, and Webflow. Lifestyle services cover housing, credit information, workouts, and tickets. Users connect apps in settings and invoke them in conversation.

Why it matters: An assistant becomes useful through access to real workflows. The important questions are what data each connection exposes and which actions it supports, not simply how many integrations exist.

Who should care: Gemini users connecting project management, creative work, and everyday planning.

Try it

Check which apps appear in your account settings. Connect one familiar tool and verify its reading and editing capabilities with a small, reviewable task.

Watch for

The rollout is gradual. Availability, subscriptions, regions, and permissions can vary by service; a connection does not establish support for every operation.

05PacttoGlobalProduct showcase 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Pactto turns creative reviews into live edits

What changed: Pactto’s September 23 product showcase presents persistent creative rooms for people and agents. Teams place video, images, PDFs, and audio on a shared canvas and discuss or annotate them together. An assistant uses asset and conversation context to suggest edits, create documents, and organize tasks, while rooms retain decisions and version information.

Why it matters: As asset creation accelerates, explaining and handing off feedback can become the bottleneck. Shared context may reduce that friction, but suggestions, actual changes, and approvals remain distinct.

Who should care: Creators, agencies, and production teams reviewing work remotely or coordinating multiple stakeholders.

Try it

Create a test room using the free plan on Pactto’s website, add your own assets, and invite a colleague. Request a specific change to one part of an asset, then compare the result with the discussion and approval status.

Watch for

The free plan retains room memory only for the latest session; cross-session memory requires Pro. These workflows have not been independently tested. Dropbox and Air are still marked as coming soon.

06AlexandriaUnited StatesProduct showcase 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Alexandria helps agents discover sources before querying

What changed: Firecrawl showcased Alexandria on September 23, bringing official APIs, licensed publishers, specialized indexes, and website connectors into one library. The current page lists 93 providers and 640 capabilities. MCP, CLI, and API access let agents discover appropriate sources and retrieve structured data beyond standard web search.

Why it matters: Useful information often sits behind specialized interfaces. Source discovery can reduce separate integrations while making provenance and pricing more visible.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams building industry research, company intelligence, government-data, and complex retrieval agents.

Try it

Choose a familiar provider from the directory. Compare returned fields, freshness, and citations with the direct official query before adding it to regular workflows.

Watch for

Indexes, official APIs, and licensed publishers provide different kinds of evidence. Capabilities may have separate prices; vendor-reported quality gains are not universal guarantees.

07ToneBirdGlobalPublished 2026-09-22 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Brief: ToneBird brings relationship context into replies

What changed: ToneBird’s Product Hunt listing is dated September 22 in Beijing time. Its Mac and Windows assistant uses earlier conversations and connected files to draft replies inside existing apps, for users to review and send.

Why it matters: Useful replies need earlier commitments and prices, not just a matching tone.

Who should care: People handling frequent client and team conversations.

Try it

Check dates, scope, and amounts using nonsensitive examples before inserting a draft.

Watch for

Website examples are not independent tests. Recalled context may be outdated.

08SpeechkaGlobalPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Brief: Hear another language in your own voice

What changed: Speechka’s September 23 showcase presents live voice translation across 44 languages, with Mac and Windows clients. The browser trial records a personal voice and lists five free minutes.

Why it matters: Translation becomes audible speech rather than only captions.

Who should care: People holding multilingual meetings, classes, or livestreams.

Try it

Choose two familiar languages and check names, numbers, and terminology.

Watch for

The browser trial requires sign-in. Published latency figures use inconsistent framing and are not independent measurements.

09MiniPlaceCanadaPublished 2026-09-21 / Observed 2026-09-24GitHub Stars 3License Apache-2.0Primary sources

Brief: Watch two agent setups reconstruct the same picture

What changed: Cohere released two MiniPlace notebooks on September 21. Peer agents or a coordinator with subagents reconstruct a 384×288 canvas, with completion requiring an exact pixel match.

Why it matters: Coordination is visible and completion has a concrete test.

Who should care: Multi-agent developers and interactive-experiment enthusiasts.

Try it

Open the local or Colab example and compare task boards, messages, and final pixel checks.

Watch for

Access to the specified Cohere models is required. Budget limits can leave a run incomplete.

10GBrainUnited StatesProduct showcase 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24GitHub Stars 30,272License MITPrimary sources

Brief: GBrain shares sourced memory across assistants

What changed: GBrain’s September 23 showcase highlights shared memory and tools. Its MIT-licensed repository supports sourced facts, corrections, and withdrawal, with local keyword search that requires no API key and optional semantic search.

Why it matters: Switching assistants need not scatter personal facts across separate chats.

Who should care: Agent users who want to manage persistent memory themselves.

Try it

Test remembering, retrieving, correcting, and withdrawing one fact before adding everyday records.

Watch for

Local storage does not mean model calls stay local. Markdown export is not a complete database backup.

11Basis Conversations 1500GlobalPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Brief: Basis releases 1,502 hours of group conversation

What changed: Basis released Conversations 1500 on September 23: 1,502 hours across 22 languages, separated by speaker, with about 100 hours of human annotations for interruptions, pauses, and responses.

Why it matters: Overlap and hesitation become accessible research data.

Who should care: Teams researching full-duplex speech and interaction.

Try it

Inspect metadata and labeled samples before downloading audio for selected languages.

Watch for

Hours count conversation time, not summed speaker tracks. Automatic transcripts are not verified training targets.

12Anthropic Life SciencesUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Brief: Claude finds a lead for laboratory follow-up

What changed: Anthropic announced its life sciences team and early findings on September 23. Claude screened public sequences and identified a candidate enzyme system associated with repeat arrays. Scientists are continuing experiments; its primary function remains unresolved.

Why it matters: Hypothesis generation and experimental validation need separate evaluation.

Who should care: Readers following AI research workflows and evidence quality.

Try it

Compare the report’s computational findings, human review, and experimental results.

Watch for

This is early research, not a validated gene-editing tool or clinical result.

13MuJoCo WarpUnited StatesPublished 2026-09-23 / Observed 2026-09-24Primary sources

Brief: A robotics tutorial scales to 2,048 environments

What changed: NVIDIA’s September 23 MJWarp tutorial moves an SO-101 scene from MuJoCo to as many as 2,048 parallel GPU environments, checking state, batching, and throughput along the way.

Why it matters: More parallel worlds do not necessarily mean lower single-step latency.

Who should care: Robotics simulation and reinforcement-learning engineers.

Try it

Validate one environment before increasing batch size and measuring aggregate throughput.

Watch for

This is a new tutorial, not a new engine release. It does not train a robot policy.
