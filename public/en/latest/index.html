The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Structured outputs

Decision Models Skip Long Answers

Decisions and Decido separate classification and scoring from prose; Craft plans visual structure first. Task-specific output does not automatically make probabilities, text, or layouts correct.

Separate permissions

Authorization Moves Beyond Models

1Password keeps credentials in a vault, Model Vault verifies inference environments, and Skills moves to neutral governance. These address sign-in, computation, and standards—not interchangeable protections.

Inspectable results

New Workflows Retain Human Checks

World exposes code diffs, MarketMee offers pre-publication review, and ForTy returns classification probabilities. Direct interaction still needs accessible evidence and clear limits.

01OpenAIUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

Decisions API Goes Live

What happened: OpenAI made the GPT-6 Luna-powered Decisions API generally available, raising the request limit from 200 questions to 400. It now accepts structured data directly, allows images within individual questions, and offers prompt caching for repeated shared input. It returns typed answers and probabilities without first generating a prose explanation.

Why it matters: High-volume classification, routing, and scoring can be separated from long-form responses. The value depends on independent questions, clear choices, and a path to human review for uncertain results—not simply on generating fewer words.

Who should care: Teams building support routing, content classification, image checks, or agent action selection.

Try it

Compare the beta and GA versions on labeled examples. Record accuracy, cache hits, and total cost. Split questions that depend on earlier answers into separate requests.

Keep in mind

The shared cache prefix needs at least 1,024 tokens; question content does not count. Confidence scores do not guarantee correctness. Input and cache writes still incur charges.

02CanvaAustralia2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedMedia reporting

Canva Craft Plans Layouts Before Generating Images

What happened: TechCrunch and The Verge reported on October 22 that Canva launched Craft, its own image model. It plans element placement, scale, hierarchy, and text before generating images at up to 2K across 30 aspect ratios. Both reports identify Dream Lab as an entry point; API and MCP expansion must be distinguished from current access.

Why it matters: Design often requires legible text and accurate relationships between elements. Two-stage generation aims to address that structure first. It can work alongside image-to-layer tools, but an image is not automatically a complete, editable design file.

Who should care: Creators and design teams making posters, social graphics, and infographics who want less layout rework.

Try it

Select Craft in Canva’s Dream Lab. Compare outputs from a prompt specifying headings, numbers, and relative positions, then check text accuracy and localized edits.

Keep in mind

These capabilities come from two publications’ launch coverage, not our testing. Up to 2K is not native 4K. Do not assume APIs or faster variants are broadly available already.

031PasswordCanada2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

1Password Handles Approved Agent Sign-Ins

What happened: 1Password announced a sign-in platform for personal agents. An agent proves its identity; the user sees the requested credential and purpose on their own device and decides whether to approve. 1Password then fills the sign-in form directly. The announcement lists several partners, but describes the Claude Duet integration as coming soon.

Why it matters: Separating sign-in approval from handing over a password can keep credentials out of model context. After signing in, however, an agent may still see account information. Login protection does not replace action permissions or approvals within the service.

Who should care: Users and product teams that want assistants to use existing accounts without giving passwords to models.

Try it

Check whether your agent’s integration is available. Use a low-risk account to inspect identity prompts, approval controls, and sign-out behavior. A partner list is not an availability list.

Keep in mind

The vendor says credentials are not exposed to the model; it does not say account contents remain invisible. Integration schedules vary, and permitted actions still need checking in each product.

04NVIDIAUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

NVIDIA Releases GR00T Apple Pick-and-Place Weights

What happened: NVIDIA’s model card dates a GR00T N1.7 apple pick-and-place checkpoint to October 22. Built for Unitree G1 and trained on 402 demonstrations, it includes three Safetensors weight shards, configurations, and normalization statistics. The name’s 15000 denotes the saved training step; 40000 denotes the cosine learning-rate schedule’s length.

Why it matters: A task-specific checkpoint lets researchers examine a specific task, dataset, and training setup rather than rely on a general demo. It is a starting point for adaptation, not evidence that arbitrary robots can reliably grasp objects.

Who should care: Developers and research teams studying embodied-model fine-tuning, robot action representations, and task reproduction.

Try it

Check the G1 inputs, action dimensions, and compatible runtime in the model card. Inspect data and normalization settings before controlled tests in an isolated environment.

Keep in mind

This task fine-tune has about 3 billion parameters; it is not a new base model. Weights use the NVIDIA Open Model Agreement, not OSI licensing.

08MarketMeeGlobal2026-10-22 demonstrated2026-10-23 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: Code Commits Become Narrated Product Videos

What happened: MarketMee demonstrated a service that turns GitHub commits into product videos, graphics, and copy. It supports narration, music, and three aspect ratios, with both review-before-publishing and automatic scheduling.

Why it matters: Release notes can become editable promotional material.

Who should care: Independent developers and small product teams.

Try it

Generate a sample from a public repository and verify every feature claim.

Keep in mind

Registration is required. Autopilot may use older commits; check its selections before connecting publishing accounts.

10QwenCloud and PixVerseChina2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: QwenCloud Adds PixVerse V6

What happened: On October 22, QwenCloud listed four PixVerse V6 endpoints: text-to-video, image-to-video, first-and-last-frame generation, and multi-subject reference generation. They support 15-second synchronized audio and video; multi-subject input accepts 2–7 images.

Why it matters: One platform now offers more ways to control generated shots.

Who should care: Developers making product showcases and short videos.

Try it

Follow the model changelog to the docs and compare keyframes with multi-image references.

Keep in mind

This is the platform integration date. The dated release note lists 2–7 images; the current reference lists 1–7. Check before calling.

11AWSUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Polly Adds 14 Generative Voices

What happened: AWS upgraded Polly’s Generative engine with 14 voices across nine locales, including its first generative Hindi and Finnish voices. Existing voice names and API calls remain unchanged.

Why it matters: Existing voice apps can compare new output without changing interfaces.

Who should care: Teams building voice assistants and narration services.

Try it

Test dates, amounts, and addresses, and check availability in your region.

Quality improvements are vendor claims. Check region and voice availability separately.

05DecidoGlobal2026-10-22 demonstrated2026-10-23 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 0License Apache-2.0

Decido Runs Structured Decisions on Laptop CPUs

What happened: Independent project Decido demonstrated a local decision model with a Jev-compatible interface. Fine-tuned from Gemma 4 26B-A4B and distributed as an 8-bit GGUF, it answers yes/no, classification, and rating questions. The installation guide targets macOS and Linux, recommends about 30GB of RAM, and limits requests to roughly 16K tokens.

Why it matters: Local document processing offers an alternative to hosted APIs, at the cost of memory and latency. Decision models still need calibration on your own examples. Returning probabilities does not make them suitable for automatic approval by default.

Who should care: Developers who need offline document classification and are willing to examine reproducibility and local deployment costs.

Try it

Start with a small labeled sample and edge cases, then vary option order. Use the supplied runtime as the model card specifies; loading the GGUF alone does not ensure calibrated outputs.

Keep in mind

The reported advantage depends on training with in-domain data; it disappears in an ablation that excludes those training splits. Some evaluation used a GPU, and the Jev scores come from a separate published run, so this is not an all-CPU, matched comparison.

06AgentIntersect WorldGlobal2026-10-22 demonstrated2026-10-23 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 1License MIT

A Code Repository Becomes a 3D City

What happened: The AgentIntersect World public preview turns a local repository into a 3D city. It connects to your installed Claude Code, Codex, Hermes, or OpenClaw harnesses, with up to four agents. Workstreams retain branches, diffs, and validation results. Descriptions can also change the scenery. Windows and Linux builds are available.

Why it matters: Spatial interfaces give work objects a location, but usefulness still depends on whether code changes are easy to inspect. World puts exploration, discussion, and execution together; it neither replaces the underlying agents nor improves their models.

Who should care: Individual developers who enjoy visual workspaces and regularly switch between coding agents.

Try it

Install the preview with a backed-up test repository. Connect one existing agent first, check that diffs and validation results are clear, then add others.

Keep in mind

This is a single-user local workspace, not a multiplayer service. Local-first does not mean the models run offline. You need your own accounts, and the Windows installer is unsigned.

09WhatYouSayGlobal2026-10-22 demonstrated2026-10-23 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 0License MIT

Brief: Voice Transcripts Return to the Original Chat

What happened: WhatYouSay published a self-hosted service that sends WhatsApp voice notes to Whisper and language models on Groq, then replies with the cleaned transcript in the same chat. Its database does not store transcript text, but retains processing metadata.

Why it matters: Read first, then decide whether to listen—without switching apps.

Who should care: Heavy voice-message users who can deploy their own service.

Try it

Read the README’s data flow and configuration, then test replies with a test account.

Keep in mind

Audio is sent to a third party. Using an unofficial WhatsApp client may trigger account restrictions.

07AnthropicUS2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Agent Skills Moves to Neutral Governance

What happened: Anthropic is donating the Agent Skills name, logo, GitHub organization, and domain to AAIF. Its proposed roadmap covers versioned releases, a formal proposal process, and security guidance. Using or contributing to the standard does not require foundation membership.

Why it matters: After cross-platform adoption, governance is moving beyond one vendor.

Who should care: Teams maintaining skills or agent platforms.

Try it

Read the specification and proposals, then check your loader’s compatibility.

Keep in mind

The roadmap is proposed; it does not mean new security mechanisms are already implemented.

12CohereCanada2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Managed Inference Adds Remote Verification

What happened: Cohere launched a public beta of Confidential Computing on Model Vault, running on NVIDIA H100 infrastructure in Azure. Clients verify hardware and software evidence before sending encrypted requests to the protected environment.

Why it matters: Protection extends beyond storage and transit to data during inference.

Who should care: AI infrastructure teams handling sensitive business information.

Try it

Read the client-verification and security-policy documentation before evaluating a test deployment.

Keep in mind

This remains a public beta. Hardware isolation guarantees neither correct outputs nor appropriate business authorization.

13Google DeepMindUK2026-10-22 published2026-10-23 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Forest Maps Track Different Kinds of Change

What happened: Google’s team published ForTy forest-typing research and an early-release time series spanning 2017–2025. The maps distinguish natural forests, plantations, and other classes, rather than treating renewed green cover as natural forest recovery.

Why it matters: Similar tree-cover totals can conceal different ecological changes.

Who should care: Readers studying land use or supply-chain environmental impacts.

Try it

Explore local time series and classification probabilities in Earth Engine.

Keep in mind

The time series is an early release. Global validation results do not replace local verification.

In perspective: Matching each task to the right interface matters more than simply adding agents. Specialized or smaller models can make decisions, explicit permissions can constrain actions, and records, layers, or probabilities can make results inspectable. Connecting those pieces could move AI beyond demonstrations and into everyday use.
