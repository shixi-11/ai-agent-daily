The RISC Machine

RISC = the four systems of a production-grade agent or robot

A production-grade agent needs more than a brain. It must keep operating, survive failure, resist overreach, and participate in real organizational workflows.

The industry has delivered an excellent brain, but a production-grade agent also needs a body, an immune system, and a social layer.ALUX is building that complete machine.
R | Resilience / BodyDurable execution, fault tolerance, recovery, and horizontal scale. Without a resilient body, a single failure can wipe out the work.
I | Intelligence / BrainModel loops, memory, tools, and orchestration. This system determines how an agent reasons, invokes tools, and completes complex work.
S | Security / Immune SystemCapability objects, policy approval, rollback, and audit. Without an immune system, recovery and connectivity expand the blast radius of failures.
C | Connectivity / Social LayerCross-company authorization, neutral substrate, session types, and ecosystem connectors. Without a social interface, an agent remains confined to a single product.

AI Agent Radar

Separate checks

Proofs and Exercises Gain Independent Checkers

AlphaProof sends agent-written proofs to Lean, while Codemm requires tests to reject wrong answers. Both separate production from acceptance.

Targeted control

Image Tools Preserve the Scope of Edits

Seedream emphasizes preserving untouched regions across revisions, while PixlPal keeps direct parameter controls. Creative tools increasingly account for what users did not ask to change.

Explicit execution

GPU and Robot Tools Clarify Connections

TLX expresses warp-group cooperation in code; ROS Portal connects allowed topics and services over a real-time network. Explicit handoffs give engineers something concrete to inspect.

01Gemini 4 CarbonUS and UK2026-11-10 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

Carbon Expands From Restricted Testing to Paying Users

What happened: Google expanded Gemini 4 Carbon from selected security defenders to paying developers and enterprises, with a rollout to AI Ultra subscribers. Antigravity is also previewing /swarm, which coordinates agents in isolated workspaces with cross-checking. Introductory public API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through November.

Why it matters: The news is broader access, not the model’s first appearance. Deeper reasoning and parallel delegation call for measuring total task time, cost, and review effort—not just the number of agents.

Who should care: Developers and enterprise teams handling difficult migrations, dense document analysis, and sustained technical research.

Try it

Try a task with acceptance criteria through the paid AI Studio API or an eligible Ultra account. For /swarm, define workspace responsibilities and inspect merged results and verification records.

Keep in mind

After the introductory period, public-model pricing is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. /swarm is a preview; cyber-specific access has separate eligibility. Benchmarks and examples are vendor disclosures, not our tests.

02MiniMax H3 ImageChina2026-11-11 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

H3 Image Releases Weights for Native 4K Generation

What happened: MiniMax released H3 Image for text-to-image generation, reference-based generation, and editing, with up to 16 reference images and 4096×4096 output. The weights were uploaded on November 10. Access is also available through the API, MiniMax Code, and Hailuo. The technical announcement is dated November 11, while the API release notes list November 10; neither date should be confused with the weight-upload timestamp.

Why it matters: High resolution and multiple references give creators more ways to work from existing subjects and assets. Local availability still requires separate checks of download size, hardware requirements, and license terms.

Who should care: Creators and developers who need high-resolution assets, multiple references, or deployable image models.

Try it

Test subject consistency with your own assets on an official service. Before deploying locally, check the model card’s VRAM and precision requirements, the download size, and the community license. Do not assume cloud speeds will carry over to your own hardware.

Keep in mind

Sub-ten-second 4K generation and speed comparisons are vendor claims, not our tests. This is a custom community license, not OSI open source. It does not establish release of the older H3 regeneration module.

03Seedream 5.5China2026-11-09 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

Seedream 5.5 Combines Iterative Editing With Transparent Assets

What happened: ByteDance Seed released Seedream 5.5, emphasizing preservation of untouched regions across edits and demonstrating 100 successive edits of one person. Native RGBA output includes partial transparency, alongside improved realism and dense text. Official trial links lead to Jimeng and Dreamina; a Pro version remains a future plan.

Why it matters: Professional work rarely ends with one image. Preserving details through revisions matters alongside first-pass quality. Transparent assets also make generated elements easier to reuse in layouts and composites.

Who should care: Designers and content teams producing commerce imagery, character assets, visual concepts, and iterative edits.

Try it

Follow the official product link, make three targeted edits to your own image, and inspect hair, gaps, and translucent edges in an exported asset before compositing.

Keep in mind

The 100-edit demonstration and text-error rates are vendor evidence, not universal guarantees. We have not confirmed a generally available public API, free quota, or downloadable weights. Product eligibility varies.

04Gemini Robotics 2.5US and UK2026-11-10 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

Robotics 2.5 Brings Dexterous Skills to a Factory Pilot

What happened: DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2.5, a vision-language-action model rolling out to robotics partners. It tested skills such as grasping, knot tying, and pipetting across over 200 tasks. An Apollo 2 automotive-kitting proof of concept at Mercedes-Benz achieved 96% success, with a separate embodied reasoning model tracking the workflow.

Why it matters: The important question is how much capability survives a move from demonstrations to a workplace. Separating workflow reasoning from arm control also helps teams locate failures at the right layer.

Who should care: Research and engineering teams working on embodied models, industrial sorting, dexterous manipulation, and robot evaluation.

Try it

Watch the factory and deformable-object demonstrations, then check hardware, task definitions, and failure conditions. Partner access does not mean downloadable weights or a public API.

Keep in mind

The 96% result comes from one proof of concept, not general factory reliability. Improvements are vendor-reported. Safety tests do not replace workplace safeguards.

06Meta Your algorithmUS2026-11-10T17:00:49Z published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

Facebook Makes Recommendation Preferences Directly Editable

What happened: Meta described Your algorithm: people edit Interested In and Not Interested In lists in natural language, backed by one preference store for Feed and Reels. New interests feed retrieval and ranking. Unwanted topics are filtered in reranking and device caches. Different language models generate profiles, validate edits, and check the final list.

Why it matters: Explicit preferences need not wait for clicks to accumulate. The useful design spans the whole pipeline: visible settings must affect ranking and invalidate content already queued on the device.

Who should care: Product and engineering teams building personalization, recommendation systems, editable memory, or preference controls.

Try it

Read the preference-management and cache-invalidation sections. Check whether your settings change the actual queue. If the feature is available to you, test one specific excluded topic.

Keep in mind

This is an architecture disclosure, not a verified worldwide rollout list. Ambiguous topics remain difficult. Layered filters and model checks do not guarantee complete removal.

08PixlPalGlobal2026-11-10T19:20:18Z demonstrated2026-11-11 observedCreator’s primary source

Brief: PixlPal Edits the Original Instead of Generating a Replacement

What happened: PixlPal translates natural-language requests into exposure, contrast, color and blur adjustments, including edits to individual objects. A manual editor remains available for fine-tuning. The website distinguishes direct editing from regenerating a whole image and offers a browser editor with a limited AI trial.

Why it matters: Connecting AI to editable controls preserves room for human revision and reduces the need to accept a newly generated scene.

Who should care: Photographers and creators who want to retain the original subject.

Try it

Open the editor and compare a local adjustment with manual fine-tuning.

Keep in mind

These are vendor demos, not our tests. The AI trial is limited, and purchases require an account.

05AlphaProof CodeUS and UK2026-11-10 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary sourceGitHub Stars 89License not declared

AlphaProof Code Extends Proofs to Protocol Implementations

What happened: DeepMind introduced AlphaProof Code, using multi-model agents to produce Lean proofs against expert-reviewed security specifications for Signal’s core cryptographic implementation. It reports over six million lines of Lean generated in four weeks. Reintroducing two previously fixed bugs into an isolated version produced checked proofs of insecurity. Formal-verification artifacts are being shared with the community.

Why it matters: The useful distinction is between a model declaring code secure and an independent checker validating its proof. Experts define the security claims; machine checking verifies the derivation.

Who should care: Researchers and engineers in formal verification, cryptographic protocols, security agents, and proof assistants.

Try it

Follow the official artifact page to the specifications, proofs, and paper. Establish the covered code and properties before trying the approach on a small module.

Keep in mind

Proofs cover specified properties and assumptions, not every bug in Signal. The main repository uses Apache-2.0; bundled libsignal retains AGPL terms. Full checking requires pinned tools; we have not rerun it.

09UmlessGlobal2026-11-10T19:18:30Z demonstrated2026-11-11 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 2License Apache-2.0

Brief: Umless Cleans Up Spoken Corrections Before Typing

What happened: Umless combines local speech recognition with a cleanup model. Hold a key to speak; release it to insert cleaned text into the active app. The project says recognition, cleanup and screen reading run on-device. It adapts phrasing to the app, while its Mac version can use visible names to improve spelling.

Why it matters: Handling a change of mind mid-sentence is different from recognizing words. A separate cleanup step targets what the speaker meant to write.

Who should care: People dictating prompts, email and messages who prefer local audio processing.

Try it

Read the requirements and model sizes, then start with the Lite source build.

Keep in mind

We reviewed documentation, not accuracy. Screen reading is Mac-only; Windows and Linux are newer and less tested.

10CodemmGlobal2026-11-10T23:11:42Z demonstrated2026-11-11 observedCreator’s primary sourceGitHub Stars 15License MIT

Brief: Codemm Validates Coding Exercises

What happened: Codemm uses your existing Claude Code or Codex account to author lessons and exercises, then checks the exercises in a sandbox. The reference solution must pass, the starter must fail, and tests must catch deliberately broken variants. Learners write their own answers while the agent provides hints and the app tracks mastery.

Why it matters: Generating an exercise is not enough. Testing whether its checks catch mistakes adds a useful quality gate before practice begins.

Who should care: Learners and educators using coding agents to explore a language or framework.

Try it

Read the validation rules and run the environment check in an empty project.

Keep in mind

Desktop builds remain beta and need an existing agent account. The anti-assistance hook is a practice aid, not a comprehensive security boundary.

07Meta TLXUS2026-11-10T16:59:31Z published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

TLX Makes GPU Cooperation Explicit in Source Code

What happened: Meta detailed TLX, a Triton extension for execution orchestration rather than a standalone language. Developers assign warp groups to move data, compute, and write results, with explicit asynchronous memory operations, barriers, and cluster cooperation. Meta reports production use for recommendations and LLM pretraining.

Why it matters: GPU performance increasingly depends on how execution groups hand off work. Making that structure visible gives engineers control over overlapping transfers and computation without waiting for compiler heuristics to catch up.

Who should care: Systems engineers optimizing training and inference kernels, using Triton, or evaluating new GPU architectures.

Try it

Compare the standard Triton and TLX matrix-multiplication examples. Mark the producers, consumers, and barriers before deciding whether your hot kernel needs explicit orchestration.

Keep in mind

This is a new technical account, not a claim that TLX was first open-sourced today. Vendor gains depend on workload and hardware. GPU execution agents are not conversational AI agents.

13Bedrock AgentCore EvaluationsUS2026-11-10T14:35:03-08:00 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Evaluate Both Individual Steps and Whole Sessions

What happened: AWS published an AgentCore evaluation guide mapping built-in, DeepEval, and AutoEval metrics to behaviors at trace and session levels. It also shows one judge call returning separate scores for multiple dimensions.

Why it matters: High completion rates can hide dropped requests or personal-data leaks.

Who should care: Teams building multi-turn support and research agents.

Try it

Start with a business outcome, choose two or three metrics, and add failure cases.

Keep in mind

This is a practice guide, not a claim that every metric launched today. Model judges need calibration.

14Vercel AI GatewayUS2026-11-09 published2026-11-11 observedOfficial primary source

Brief: Decision Models Take Many Requests but Little Spend

What happened: Vercel’s November report says decision models received 26% of October AI Gateway requests but just 0.53% of estimated list-price spend. About a third of paid teams used these classification or selection models.

Why it matters: Generating answers and making quick decisions have different cost structures.

Who should care: Teams designing model routing and inference budgets.

Try it

Read the methodology, then compare a specialist model with a general model on one classification task.

Keep in mind

The sample covers this gateway only. Spend uses public list prices, not industry-wide bills.

In perspective: Across frontier models and small tools, the useful pattern is consistent: define intent, specify what may change, check the result independently, and then decide which actions deserve further automation.
